# Writer draft — 2026-11-17

# Should Your AI Team Act First, or Wait for Your Brief?

Two platforms in this category deserve a side-by-side on the same question, because they answer it oppositely. Marblism sells seven named AI employees under a flat subscription and markets them as proactive: they follow up, they initiate, they act when something happens, not when you are at your desk. Kohai sells a team of named colleagues you brief like a hire: one message is the whole job description, and anything sensitive waits at an approval gate until you say go.

If you are a solo operator or a lean team, that difference is not cosmetic. It decides how your first month actually feels.

## What Marblism Gets Right

Start with what Marblism earns, because it earns a lot. A flat subscription covering every employee is the simplest billing in the category: as of this writing, $44 a month on monthly billing, or $24 a month billed yearly, for all seven employees and 50 hours of AI work per month. Unlimited team members, no per-seat fees. Chatting with the employees and configuring them is free. Hours reset monthly, and higher tiers bring the per-hour cost down for heavy lead-gen, content, or call workloads.

The employees have real shapes. Eva sorts and drafts your inbox. Rachel answers your phone around the clock. Stan runs sales outreach and lead enrichment. Penny produces SEO content. That concreteness is why the platform has pulled in more than 40,000 small and mid-sized businesses since launching, with a 4.8 out of 5 rating on Trustpilot.

And Marblism is honest about its control model. Its own customer data shows owners approve 9 in 10 of the drafts they act on. Proactive does not mean unsupervised there. Give them credit for that.

## The Question Behind the Marketing

Every AI-employee platform has to answer: who starts the work?

The proactive-first answer is: the employees do. Work starts when a trigger fires, an email lands, a lead needs a follow-up. That is a genuine advantage when coverage is your problem. The enquiry that arrives Sunday night gets handled Sunday night, and your inbox is sorted before you open your eyes. If your pain is "things sit until I have a spare hour," proactive is aimed at you.

The brief-first answer is: you do, once. You describe the outcome, your colleague works, the finished deliverable lands in your Library. The first deliverable arrives the same day. Nothing moves on your accounts until the next brief.

Neither is a trick. But they produce different weeks, and the difference compounds.

## What Proactive Costs, Honestly

An AI that acts first needs a boundary you trust, because its initiative is aimed at your real accounts: your customers, your leads, your books.

Marblism handles this with human review: nine of ten drafts get owner approval, which is a healthy discipline and, read carefully, also a workload. Approval is free in their billing, but it is not free in your time. If the employees initiate broadly, your daily review grows with their activity. That is the deal, and some owners will love it.

Kohai takes a different position: initiative stays inside a scoped boundary, and the boundary is explicit. "~1,000 integrations connected day one, with per-agent tool scoping." Each colleague can only touch the tools you scoped to that role. Sensitive actions do not just get flagged for review later; they wait at an approval gate until you say go. You stay in control, and the control is structural rather than a habit you have to keep.

So the honest comparison is not "proactive vs passive." It is "proactive with a review queue" versus "proactive within a perimeter, gated where it counts." Kohai's colleagues are proactive too: schedules and automations are set once and run without a daily prompt. The difference is that everything outside your guardrails stops and asks.

## What the Two Bills Look Like

Both platforms meter usage, but they meter different things.

Marblism meters time. Every task draws from a monthly hour balance: an email draft is five minutes, a social post is thirty, a blog post is an hour, a handled call is ten minutes. Chat is free. Hours are intuitive because they read like payroll, and running out just means moving up a tier.

Kohai meters task size in credits, with published costs: a blog post is 20 to 50 credits, a deck or deep research is 100 to 300, small asks are 5 to 10. Two details matter here. Failed tasks consume zero credits, so a botched run costs you nothing. And add-on credits never expire. For deep, spiky work, task-size pricing has one quiet advantage over hours: a four-hour research spiral cannot silently eat your month, because the task is priced, not the clock.

Current Kohai pricing, verified on the pricing page as of October 19, 2026: Kōhai Free at $0 with no card and credits refreshing monthly (limited time, first 1,000 users keep it), Kōhai at $20/month with 8,000 credits, Kōhai Pro at $100/month with 40,000 credits, Kōhai Max at $200/month with 80,000 credits. Unlimited seats on every plan. Marblism's entry price is lower on monthly billing; Kohai's Pro tier carries the credit volume of a full content and outreach operation, with unlimited seats included rather than per-seat.

## Which One Should You Pick

Choose Marblism if round-the-clock coverage is your bottleneck: a phone that never rings into the void, an inbox that sorts itself, a flat bill you barely think about. It is a strong product, and it will not ask you to build anything.

Choose Kohai if your bottleneck is finished work you can trust: deliverables in a Library, not chat transcripts. Software that gets built, deployed, and hosted, not mocked up. Colleagues who act, but only inside a perimeter you drew, with an approval gate between them and anything sensitive.

One of them starts the work for you. The other lets you decide when the work starts, and keeps the pen in your hand when it does.
