## Fix 1 — HIGH severity: money-keyword pages have slug–title mismatch ("AI employee" appears in URLs but never in titles)

**What I found:** Five URLs are built on the head-term slugs searchers actually type — `what-is-an-ai-employee`, `best-ai-agents-for-small-business`, `how-ai-employees-help-every-company-size`, `ai-employee-vs-virtual-assistant-vs-freelancer`, `ai-for-small-business` — but every title tag and meta description has been rewritten to the brand voice ("AI teammate(s)"):

| URL | Current title | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| /blog/what-is-an-ai-employee | "What is an AI teammate?" | Slug targets "what is an AI employee"; title targets a different phrase |
| /blog/best-ai-agents-for-small-business | "Comparing AI teams for small business…" | Slug targets "best AI agents for small business"; title says "AI teams" |
| /blog/how-ai-employees-help-every-company-size | "How AI teammates help companies of every size" | Same pattern |
| /blog/ai-employee-vs-virtual-assistant-vs-freelancer | "AI teammates vs. virtual assistants…" | Same pattern |
| /blog/best-ai-tools-for-business | (not fetched in title pass) | Likely same — verify |

Why it matters: title tags are the strongest on-page relevance signal for the head terms 04-content-strategy.md explicitly names as money keywords ("AI employees for small business", "hire AI employees"). The slug alone is a weak signal, and AI engines quote titles when answering "what is Kohai" — right now they'd quote "AI teammate", a term with a fraction of the search/answer volume. The brand-voice rule ("colleagues, not agents") applies to prose, not the metadata surface: keeping the search term in the `<title>` while the H1 stays in-voice is standard practice and doesn't compromise positioning.

**Copy-paste fixes (title + meta description):**

1. `/blog/what-is-an-ai-employee`
```
<title>What is an AI employee? (+ how an AI teammate works) | Kōhai Blog</title>
<meta name="description" content="An AI employee is software with a role, the right tools, memory and accountability. What that means in practice, and how it differs from a chatbot.">
```
2. `/blog/best-ai-agents-for-small-business`
```
<title>Best AI agents for small business in 2026: an honest comparison | Kōhai Blog</title>
<meta name="description" content="We compared the leading AI agents and AI employees for small businesses on what they do, how they charge and who each is right for.">
```
3. `/blog/how-ai-employees-help-every-company-size`
```
<title>How AI employees help companies of every size | Kōhai Blog</title>
<meta name="description" content="AI employees look different at one person, 10 people, 50 people and 1,000. What they do at each scale, with the boring details included.">
```
4. `/blog/ai-employee-vs-virtual-assistant-vs-freelancer`
```
<title>AI employee vs. virtual assistant vs. freelancer: which fits your business? | Kōhai Blog</title>
<meta name="description" content="An honest comparison of the three options every solopreneur faces: costs, strengths, where each one wins and when a hybrid setup makes sense.">
```

Implementation note: title tags only — leave H1s and body copy in the "teammate" voice. Slugs stay as-is (don't redirect; the slugs are already correct).

## Fix 2 — HIGH-MED severity: /pricing ships zero pricing schema (no Product/Offer, no FAQPage) while the page has both, ready

**What I found:** The pricing page carries full FAQ content ("What's a credit?", "What's the difference between the plans?", "Is there an annual plan?") and four concrete prices — but its JSON-LD is only the site-wide Organization + WebSite. No `Product`/`Offer`, no `FAQPage`, no `BlogPosting` on blog posts either (checked: only Organization/WebSite/ImageObject anywhere).

Why it matters: the documented pain (stale $29.99/$99.99 figures still circulating in third-party content and the old digest section) is exactly what schema solves — engines are far likelier to cite the current $20/$100/$200 numbers when they're machine-readable rather than inferred from prose. This is the cheapest insurance for the 10 AI visibility audit prompts that hinge on pricing accuracy ("Cheapest AI workforce platform with predictable pricing").

**Copy-paste snippet — add to `/pricing` `<head>` (prices verified live 2026-10-31; confirm with Gabe before shipping since it re-asserts pricing):**
```json
<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "FAQPage",
  "mainEntity": [
    {"@type": "Question", "name": "What's the difference between the Kōhai plans?", "acceptedAnswer": {"@type": "Answer", "text": "Credits. Kōhai includes 8,000 credits a month, Kōhai Pro 40,000 and Kōhai Max 80,000. Every plan includes unlimited team seats, the whole AI team, every role template and every integration."}},
    {"@type": "Question", "name": "Is there a free plan?", "acceptedAnswer": {"@type": "Answer", "text": "Yes, for a limited time. Kōhai Free is open to the first 1,000 users, needs no card and includes credits that refresh monthly."}},
    {"@type": "Question", "name": "Is there an annual plan?", "acceptedAnswer": {"@type": "Answer", "text": "Yes: $220/year for Kōhai, $1,050/year for Kōhai Pro and $2,100/year for Kōhai Max."}},
    {"@type": "Question", "name": "What can you do with Kōhai's credits?", "acceptedAnswer": {"@type": "Answer", "text": "Small tasks (a quick research query, a short email) use 5-10 credits. Medium tasks (a chart, a landing page, a blog post) use 20-50. Large jobs (a deck, market research) use 100-300. Failed tasks consume 0 credits."}}
  ]
}
</script>
```
Plus a `Product`/`Offer` block (one entry per tier, `priceCurrency: "USD"`, prices 20/100/200 monthly, availability `InStock`, with `Kōhai Free` as a $0 offer marked limited-time) — same `<head>`. Secondary item in the same fix: wrap blog posts in `Article`/`BlogPosting` schema.
