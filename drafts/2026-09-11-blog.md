# How to delegate ops to AI: the Monday growth report, fully automated

Every founder has one task that eats Monday morning. For most, it's the growth report: pull last week's numbers, check them against the week before, write up what moved and why, paste it into a doc, send it to the team or to yourself.

It's not hard work. It's just work — every week, forever. And it's the best place to start if you want to delegate ops to AI, because it has everything delegation needs: a clear outcome, recurring timing, and data that already lives in your tools.

Here's how to hand it off completely, using the playbook we run on Kohai.

## Step 1: brief the outcome, not the steps

The mistake most people make with AI operations is writing instructions. If you have to specify every data source, every field, every formatting rule — you haven't delegated anything. You've just moved the work onto a worse keyboard.

Brief it the way you'd brief a capable hire: "Every Monday at 9:00, pull last week's growth numbers and write the report. Flag anything that changed more than usual."

That one message is the whole job description. In Kohai, your Chief of Staff — Mochi — takes the brief, splits it into tasks, and hands each piece to the right specialist. The Strategy Lead pulls metrics and builds dashboards. The Operations Lead handles status reporting and scheduling. You never touch the plumbing.

## Step 2: put it on a schedule

Recurring work should run without you re-asking. On Kohai, you set a schedule once — "Mon 9:00 Growth report" — and it runs weekly. Event triggers handle the less predictable stuff: a new enterprise deal, a spike in support tickets, a campaign that needs a follow-up.

This is the difference between a demo and a system. Anyone can get an AI to write one report. The question is whether it happens again next Monday while you're doing something else.

A solo operator's week has a handful of these recurring jobs. A useful starting set:

| Recurring job | Schedule | Who handles it |
|---|---|---|
| Growth report | Mon 9:00 | Strategy Lead |
| Pipeline sweep | Wed 14:00 | Sales & Growth Lead |
| Invoice chase | Fri 16:00 | Operations Lead |
| Support triage | Around the clock | Customer Support Lead |

Notice the invoice chase. Some jobs shouldn't run without you watching.

## Step 3: approve what's sensitive, ignore the rest

Delegation fails when it forces a choice between two bad options: check everything the AI does, or check nothing.

Kohai takes a third path. Sensitive actions wait at an approval gate until you say go. The report gets written and delivered; the invoice-chase email sits in a queue for your approval before it sends. You stay in control of the moments with consequences and ignore the rest.

This matters more than it sounds. Trust in a colleague isn't built on watching their every keystroke — it's built on knowing which doors they can't open without you. Per-agent tool scoping works the same way: each teammate only touches the tools you allow, out of roughly 1,000 integrations connected on day one.

## What a fully delegated Monday looks like

You open your laptop Monday at 9:15. The report is already in your Library — last week's numbers, the movement, the draft commentary. You read it with coffee, mark two edits, and approve the follow-up email it drafted for the deal that went quiet.

Nothing was "generated" at you. Work was finished and delivered, the way a good operations person would deliver it.

The hours add up faster than you'd expect. In an example week on Kohai: 24 deliverables shipped and 31 hours reclaimed across sales outreach, content, ops reporting, and support. The Monday report is one line item in that number — but it's usually the one that convinces people the rest is possible.

## What it costs

Credits, not tokens — you can see what a piece of work costs before you assign it. Plans start at $29.99/mo for 12,000 credits (1 seat), with Pro at $99.99/mo for 40,000 credits and 3 seats, and Max at $199.99/mo for 80,000 credits and unlimited seats. Every plan includes a 7-day free trial, and the first deliverable lands in your Library the same day.

If you're a solo founder, the arithmetic to run is simple: what does the Monday report cost you today in time, multiplied by every week you'll keep doing it? Set that against $29.99 and a schedule you set once.

## Start with one report

Don't delegate your whole operation on day one. Pick the one recurring task that reliably ruins a morning — for most people it's the Monday report — and hand that off completely: brief the outcome, set the schedule, define what's sensitive.

When it shows up finished next Monday without your involvement, the second and third playbooks will feel obvious. Pipeline sweeps, invoice chases, support triage — same pattern, different brief.

Brief it like a colleague. Approve what matters. Let Monday be yours again.
