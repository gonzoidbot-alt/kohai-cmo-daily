# Marblism vs Kohai: Flat Plan or Per-Deliverable Credits?

**Target query:** Marblism alternative
**Meta description:** Both Marblism and Kohai put an AI team to work without building agents. The pricing shapes differ — one flat plan, one per deliverable. How to choose.

You've decided against another agent builder. You don't want to construct workflows, wire triggers, or maintain automation you'll have to debug at 11 p.m. You want to describe an outcome and have a team produce the work. Two names keep coming up in that search: Marblism and Kohai.

Both are task-first platforms. Neither asks you to build anything. So the decision comes down to three things: how you're billed, how work starts, and what arrives on day one. Here's each one, plainly.

## What do the two platforms actually sell?

Marblism staffs you a set of AI employees under one flat plan and frames the work in hours. Its pitch: the whole team costs less than a coffee a day and replaces payroll it sizes at $2,000–$10,000 a month. Kohai gives you named leads — Strategy, Sales & Growth, Marketing & Comms, Customer Support, Operations — and prices the work per deliverable, in credits. Deliverables land in a Library, and anything sensitive waits at an approval gate until you say go.

Same category, different operating assumptions. Marblism assumes the team should run itself. Kohai assumes you should brief it, then approve the parts that touch the outside world.

## How do the pricing shapes differ?

| | Marblism | Kohai |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | One flat plan, every employee included | Four tiers, credits per deliverable |
| Framing | Hours-based ("less than a coffee a day") | Per-task credit quotes (a blog post: 20–50 credits; a deck: 100–300) |
| Seats | Included in the flat plan | Unlimited seats on every plan |
| Entry point | A single low flat monthly plan | Free plan at $0 (limited time, no card), then $20 / $100 / $200 per month |

Marblism's flat plan is the simplest possible answer to "what will this cost me?" One number. Every employee. Done. If your workload is steady and uniform, that simplicity is real value — you never think about metering.

Kohai's credit model answers a different question: "what did each piece of work cost?" A blog post quotes in the 20–50 range, a deck in the 100–300 range, and a failed task consumes zero credits. Credits refresh monthly; add-on credits never expire. If your workload spikes — a launch week followed by a quiet one — per-deliverable pricing scales down with you, and the per-task quote tells you what a request costs *before* you assign it.

One honest complication on the flat side: "flat" prices the team, not the workload. If your hours-used climbs month over month, ask what happens at the ceiling — overage terms, throttling, or an upsell. On the credit side, the equivalent question is the opposite: are you buying more capacity than a quiet month consumes? Both are fair questions. Ask them of any vendor in this category.

## How does the work start?

This is the sharpest philosophical difference between the two, and it deserves more weight than the price tag.

Marblism positions its employees as proactive — they act first, without waiting for your prompt. That's a genuine strength if what you fear is work that *didn't happen*: the inbox answered, the posts published, the follow-ups sent while you slept.

Kohai is brief-first. You describe the outcome, the colleague produces the work, and anything sensitive — a send, a payment, a publish — waits at an approval gate until you say go. That's the strength if what you fear is work that happened *without you*: unsupervised outbound, a message you'd have phrased differently, a charge you'd have questioned.

Neither is wrong. But they optimize for different failures. Before you compare anything else, answer this honestly: which mistake would cost you more — the task nobody picked up, or the action nobody cleared with you? Your answer picks your platform shape.

## What's there on day one?

Marblism covers a channel Kohai doesn't advertise: voice. Its phone employee, Rachel, answers calls — real inbound coverage for an owner who lives on the phone.

Kohai's day-one breadth runs in a different direction: "~1,000 integrations connected day one, with per-agent tool scoping." The second half of that sentence matters as much as the first when you're comparing platforms. Per-agent scoping means each colleague touches only the tools you scoped to it — your Support Lead sees the support stack, not your books. That's what keeps an AI team auditable, and it's the mechanism behind the approval gates above.

Both plans include everything on their side: Marblism's flat plan includes every employee; every Kohai tier includes the whole team, every role template, and every integration, with unlimited seats.

## Where does your data go?

If your work touches client data — finance, legal, healthcare-adjacent services — make the model question decisive. Kohai runs exclusively on open-weight models (DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM) from inference providers certified not to train on your data, with per-tenant isolation and encryption. Whatever platform you're evaluating, ask each vendor the same two questions directly: where does inference run, and is provider training on your data contractually excluded? Write the answers down. A platform that can't answer both in one sentence has answered anyway.

## Which one fits you?

1. **One steady monthly number, a team that runs itself, phone coverage** — Marblism's shape fits. Its flat plan and proactive framing are built for that owner.
2. **A spiky workload, per-deliverable cost visibility, and a gate before anything sensitive ships** — Kohai's shape fits. Start on the Free plan at $0, no card, credits refreshing monthly — limited time — and see what a week of briefed work actually produces before a dollar is committed.
3. **Client data in the work** — make the data-handling answers above the deciding row, whichever way you lean.
4. **Still unsure** — pick on the work-start question, not the feature list. The billing shape and the control model are the two things you can't change about a platform by using it more.

Both platforms are honest answers to the same problem: work that needs doing and a team that's too small to do it. The right one is the one whose defaults match how much control you want and how predictable your month is. Choose on those, and the feature lists take care of themselves.
