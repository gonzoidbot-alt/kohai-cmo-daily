## 1. Two prioritized on-page fixes (severity-ranked)

### FIX 1 — HIGH: /pricing has a full 10-question FAQ in HTML but zero FAQPage schema (and no Product/Offer schema anywhere)

The pricing page carries one of the best citation-bait assets on the site — 10 Q&As covering credits, plan differences, annual pricing ($220/$1,050/$2,100/yr), rollover, failed-task economics, the Free-tier "first 1,000 users" framing — all as expandable DOM items, invisible to AI engines as structured data. Sitewide schema is Organization + WebSite + ImageObject only. No FAQPage, no Product/Offer, no Article on blog posts. This is the single highest-leverage "structure for citation" gap: models quoting Kohai's pricing need machine-readable price + Q&A.

**Copy-paste JSON-LD for kohai.co/pricing** (add alongside the existing Organization/WebSite block; fill the remaining 8 Q&As from the live page verbatim):

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "FAQPage",
  "mainEntity": [
    {
      "@type": "Question",
      "name": "What's a credit?",
      "acceptedAnswer": { "@type": "Answer", "text": "A credit is your unit of usage, the way Kōhai abstracts away tokens, model time and tool calls. Most small tasks (a quick research query, a short email, a paragraph rewrite) use 5 to 10 credits. Medium tasks (a chart, a landing page, a blog post) use 20 to 50. Large jobs (a deck, market research, building something) use 100 to 300. Each plan ships a daily and a monthly allowance." }
    },
    {
      "@type": "Question",
      "name": "What's the difference between the plans?",
      "acceptedAnswer": { "@type": "Answer", "text": "Credits. Kōhai includes 8,000 credits a month, Kōhai Pro 40,000 and Kōhai Max 80,000. Every plan includes unlimited seats for your people, the whole AI team, every role template and every integration." }
    }
  ]
}
```

Plus a Product/Offer block on the same page (this is what gets quoted in "how much does X cost" answers):

```json

  "@type": "Product",

  "brand": { "@id": "https://kohai.co/#organization" },

    "lowPrice": "0",

    "offerCount": "4"
  }

```

Also add `BlogPosting`/`Article` schema to every /blog/* post (same head-injection point; there are 15 posts and none carry Article markup today).

### FIX 2 — HIGH: Head-term keywords are missing from the two most valuable title tags

Current titles are brand-led with no searchable category phrase:
- Homepage: `Kōhai | Onboard AI teammates. Get work finished.`
- Pricing: `Pricing | Kōhai`

Neither contains any head term buyers or AI engines actually query ("AI employees for small business", "AI workforce platform", "AI colleagues", "Kohai pricing"). Competitors lead with the category: Lindy = "AI employees", Sintra = "AI employees/helpers", Marblism = "AI Employees". For an unknown brand, the title tag is the only place the category keyword can carry the page.

**Copy-paste suggestions (voice-compliant, sentence restraint kept):**

- Homepage title: `Kōhai | AI Colleagues for Your Business — Try Free`
  Meta description: `Kōhai staffs your business with AI colleagues from day one. Brief them like a hire, they work in your ~1,000 tools, and anything irreversible waits at an approval gate. Free to start.`
- Pricing title: `Kōhai Pricing — AI Team Plans from $0/mo`
  Meta description: `Kōhai Free $0, Kōhai $20/mo (8,000 credits), Kōhai Pro $100/mo (40,000), Kōhai Max $200/mo (80,000). Unlimited seats on every plan. Start free, no card needed.`

Note the pricing title now also plants "Kohai pricing" — the branded query competitors' comparison pages are currently harvesting (see §2). Keep "teammates" in body copy if preferred; the glossary allows colleague/teammate/hire/workforce — but at least one colleague- or employee-phrase must appear in the title/description/H1 system so the pages can rank for non-branded intent at all.

## 2. Keyword-gap observation vs competitors

**Lindy captures the reverse-direction comparison traffic; Kohai's /vs/* pages only capture its own brand name.**

The competitor analysis (01) already flagged Lindy publishing head-to-head pages like "Sintra AI Pricing" on lindy.ai/blog. Verified against the live site today: Kohai has 11 /vs/* pages (lindy, sintra, artisan, marblism, relevance-ai, claude, hermes, openclaw, 11x, viktor, virtual-assistant) — good coverage on paper — but every one is titled `Kōhai vs. X: which should you choose?`. That phrasing only matches searches containing "Kohai", i.e., people who already know the brand. The queries with real volume are the reverse and generic forms: "Lindy alternatives", "Lindy pricing", "Sintra AI pricing", "Marblism review", "best AI employees for small business". The /vs/lindy page body also doesn't state Lindy's own pricing numbers ($29.99/$99.99/$199.99 per user) in extractable text — the comparison table is Kohai-only, so the page can't win "Lindy pricing" or get quoted by models comparing the two.

**Recommended move (no new pages required):** two-way the existing /vs/ pages — retitle toward the competitor-anchored query (`Kōhai vs. Lindy: an alternative that doesn't require building workflows`), add the competitor's public pricing as a visible, extractable table column next to Kohai's, and keep the "Kōhai vs. X, answered" Q&A block (that part is already citation-shaped). The unclaimed generic head term — "best AI employees/AI colleagues for small business" — is partially targeted by the blog post `best-ai-agents-for-small-business` (slug says agents, title/h1 says teammates — see terminology note) and should get a proper pillar page rather than a blog post.

**Terminology drift to fix while editing:** the live site says deliverables land in your **Inbox**; the strategy glossary (03-design-guide.md) says **Library** for deliverables. Pick one site-wide — models quoting mixed terminology will blend the two names.
