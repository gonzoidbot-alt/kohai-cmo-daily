## Fix 1 — HIGH: Homepage title/meta/H2 carry zero money keywords

**Problem.** The homepage — the strongest page on the domain — targets nothing searchers type. The 04-content-strategy money phrases ("AI employees for small business", "AI workforce platform", "hire AI employees") appear nowhere in the homepage title, meta description, H1, or any of its 9 H2s.

- Current title (48 chars, half wasted on brand-first + a sentence): `Kōhai | Onboard AI teammates. Get work finished.`
- Current meta: describes the mechanic ("collaborative AI workspace… fully staffed") with no query terms.
- "Workforce" appears in **zero** page titles site-wide (checked: home, pricing, features, vs, blog, both solutions pages). The one phrase the strategy names as a money page has no page owning it.

**Fix (copy-paste, homepage `<head>`):**


## Fix 2 — MEDIUM-HIGH: /pricing has FAQ content but no FAQPage schema (and no Offer markup)

**Problem.** The pricing page has five well-formed FAQ H3s ("What's a credit?", "What's the difference between the plans?", "Is there an annual plan?", "Is there a free plan?", "I started a trial. What happens now?") with clean, quotable answers — and zero structured data. Grep of the full HTML confirms no FAQPage, Product, or Offer schema anywhere on the page. That answer text is exactly what AI answer engines quote when buyers ask "cheapest AI workforce platform with predictable pricing" (audit prompt #5 in the GTM playbook) — right now it's unmarked prose.

Honest note: Google retired FAQ rich results for most sites in 2023, so this won't buy stars in Google SERPs. The value is LLM/answer-engine grounding and clean machine-readable pricing — which is the GEO half of the strategy.

**Fix (copy-paste, insert before `</head>` or before `</body>` on /pricing — answers verbatim from the live page):**

```html
<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "FAQPage",
  "mainEntity": [
    {
      "@type": "Question",
      "name": "What's the difference between the plans?",
      "acceptedAnswer": { "@type": "Answer", "text": "Credits. Kōhai includes 8,000 credits a month, Kōhai Pro 40,000 and Kōhai Max 80,000. Every plan includes unlimited seats for your people, the whole AI team, every role template and every integration." }
    },
    {
      "@type": "Question",
      "name": "Is there an annual plan?",
      "acceptedAnswer": { "@type": "Answer", "text": "Yes: $220/year for Kōhai, $1,050/year for Kōhai Pro and $2,100/year for Kōhai Max. Choose annual billing in the app." }
    },
    {
      "@type": "Question",
      "name": "Is there a free plan?",
      "acceptedAnswer": { "@type": "Answer", "text": "Yes, for a limited time. Kōhai Free is open to the first 1,000 users, needs no card and includes credits that refresh every month. Join while spots remain and you keep it." }
    }
  ]
}
</script>
```

Add the remaining two Q&As ("What's a credit?", trial question) from the live page copy — answers exist verbatim, they only need wrapping. Do not edit answer text; schema must match on-page content.

## Keyword-gap observation: "AI teammates" is brand vocabulary nobody searches — "AI employees" is the query, and it's orphaned

The site says "AI teammates" everywhere — homepage H1, blog explainer title ("What is an AI teammate?"), features, solutions. It's a real differentiator and the right brand term. But it's also a zero-volume phrase: searchers type "AI employee" / "AI employees" / "AI workforce". The site has exactly one page adjacent to that query (blog/what-is-an-ai-employee — targeting the teammate variant) and no page whose title contains "AI workforce" at all, despite 04-content-strategy naming "AI workforce platform" as a money page and 06-gtm-funnel-playbook listing "How do I hire an AI workforce instead of building agents?" as a monthly audit prompt.

Two moves, no rebrand required:

1. **Bridge the terms in copy** (copy-paste one-liner for the homepage sub-H1 area or features intro):
   > "Your AI teammates — the AI employees, agents and assistants other tools make you build — arrive hired."

   Pairing the searched term with the brand term in the same sentence captures the query without abandoning the vocabulary that makes the product distinct.
2. **Own the money phrase with one page.** Either rename /solutions/smb to target "AI employees for small business" (title: `AI Employees for Small Businesses | Kōhai`) or ship the already-planned GEO anchor page on that exact phrase. Competitors own this space today: Sintra ranks on "AI employees" positioning, Lindy publishes head-to-head pricing takedowns, and Kohai's own /vs hub is the right chassis to feed it.
