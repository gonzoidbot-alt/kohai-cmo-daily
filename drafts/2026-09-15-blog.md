# How to delegate ops to AI: a founder's playbook

Every founder we talk to has the same three tasks on their hate list: the Monday report, the invoice chase, and the support inbox. None of them is hard. All of them eat the hours you'd rather spend on customers and product.

Delegating them to AI is possible today, and it doesn't require building a single agent. But most founders who try it quit in week two, for a predictable reason: they delegate the way an engineer would, not the way a manager would. This post is the manager's version — the same playbook we'd walk through with you on a call, written down.

## Step 1: Delegate an outcome, not a task list

The instinct is to write instructions: "check analytics, pull pipeline, summarize tickets, format the doc." That's task-list thinking, and it hands you back a second job — supervising the steps.

Delegate the outcome instead. One message, the way you'd brief a capable hire on their first week: "Every Monday at 9:00, send me a growth report covering last week's pipeline, content numbers, and support load." You didn't specify how. You don't need to. The whole job description is that one sentence.

If you find yourself writing more than three sentences, you're still designing a workflow. Cut it back to the outcome and who it's for.

## Step 2: Let a chief of staff split the work

A single brief usually touches several jobs. A growth report needs numbers pulled, a summary written, and a slack message sent — research, writing, and ops in one ask.

This is where an AI chief of staff earns the title. On Kohai, Mochi takes your brief, splits it into tasks, and hands each one to the right specialist — a Strategy Lead for the metrics, an Operations Lead for the reporting. The tools are already connected; nobody wires an integration in the middle of the work. Kohai connects to about a thousand apps on day one — Gmail, Slack, Notion, Stripe, HubSpot, Sheets among them — with per-agent tool scoping, so each hire only touches what you allow.

That last part matters more than it sounds. Delegation fails when the new hire can see everything and you have to trust all of it. Scoped tools flip that: you decide what the Sales Lead can reach and what the Ops Lead can reach, separately. "~1,000 integrations connected day one, with per-agent tool scoping" is the feature, but the benefit is that you never have to choose between capability and control.

## Step 3: Put the sensitive parts behind an approval gate

Not every action should run unattended. Sending the invoice reminder to your biggest client, replying to a furious customer, spending money — those should wait for you.

Approval gates are how you delegate without abdicating. You mark which actions are sensitive, and anything in that category queues until you say go. Everything else finishes on its own and lands in your Library as a finished deliverable — the report, the drafted email, the updated spreadsheet — not a chat transcript you have to assemble yourself.

This is the part most "autonomous agent" marketing gets backwards. The dream isn't an AI that never asks. The dream is an AI that knows what to ask about. You stay in the loop where it counts and out of it everywhere else.

## Step 4: Set it once, then judge it weekly

A delegated process isn't done until it recurs. Schedule it — Monday 9:00 for the growth report, Friday 16:00 for the invoice chase — and let it run without you re-briefing each week.

Then manage it like you'd manage a person: by output. Two numbers are enough. Deliverables shipped this week, and hours the work would have taken you by hand. On Kohai's example workspace, that's 24 deliverables a week and 31 hours reclaimed — 11 in sales outreach, 8 in content and SEO, 7 in ops and reporting, 5 in support triage. Your numbers will differ. The point is that they're countable, and week over week, the count is what tells you whether the delegation is working.

If the output is wrong, don't debug anything. Re-brief, the way you'd redirect a person: "skip the content section until we launch, add churn by cohort." The fix is a sentence, not a configuration.

## A worked example: the Monday report, end to end

Here's the whole loop for the task most founders hand off first:

1. **Monday 8:55** — you're at coffee. The report is already being assembled.
2. **Monday 9:00** — it lands in your Library: pipeline from the CRM, content numbers from analytics, support summary from the ticket queue, written up in your format.
3. **Monday 9:02** — you read it in four minutes. One follow-up: "add last month's comparison." Done by 9:05.

Total founder time: about ten minutes, none of it setup. The first week includes the one-time cost of checking the output is right — after that, the recurring brief runs itself.

## What this doesn't require

No prompt engineering. No workflow diagrams. No agent-building platform where you become the architect — if you enjoy that craft, tools like Lindy and Relevance AI are good at it, and they're built for people who do. But a founder delegating ops doesn't want to become an automation engineer; they want the report to exist. Hire, don't build.
