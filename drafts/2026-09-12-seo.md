## Fix #1 — HIGH: Three competing assistant identities on one site ("Track", "Alfred", "Mochi")

**Problem.** The homepage — the page with the most authority and the first thing both Googlebot and AI crawlers read — still demos the product with **legacy names**:

- "Track, the lead agent" (homepage 3-step demo, step 2)
- "Alfred, your AI personal assistant" (homepage meeting-flow section)
- "Alfred · Pulling last deck, pipeline numbers, and attendee notes"

Meanwhile /features, /pricing, /solutions/*, and /vs/* all use the current naming: **"Mochi, your Chief of Staff."** The strategy docs (00-product-info.md) confirm Mochi is the current name and "Track" is the retired one.

Why this is an SEO/GEO problem, not just cosmetic:

1. **Entity split.** AI engines asked "What is Kohai?" or "Who is Mochi?" get contradictory signals — one page says the chief-of-staff product is Mochi, the homepage says it's "Alfred" and the lead agent is "Track." The 04-content-strategy.md GEO goal ("AI engines describe Kohai as 'AI colleagues/workforce platform with approval gates and open-weight models'") depends on a single coherent entity.
2. **Design-guide violation.** 03-design-guide.md: use "Mochi, your Chief of Staff", avoid "the assistant" register. "Alfred, your AI personal assistant" is exactly the register the guide bans — and it's generic enough that AI engines can't bind it to the Kohai entity at all.
3. **"Lead agent" vocabulary** reintroduces the "agent" framing the entire positioning (02: "colleagues, not agents") is built against — on the homepage, of all pages.

**Copy-paste fixes (homepage strings):**

```text
"Track, the lead agent, splits your goal into tasks..."
→ "Mochi, your Chief of Staff, splits your goal into tasks..."

"## Alfred preps, briefs, takes notes — then does the follow-ups."
→ "## Mochi preps, briefs, takes notes — then does the follow-ups."

"Alfred, your AI personal assistant, preps your meetings..."
→ "Mochi, your Chief of Staff, preps your meetings..."

"Alfred · Pulling last deck, pipeline numbers, and attendee notes"
→ "Mochi · Pulling last deck, pipeline numbers, and attendee notes"

"Track is assembling your team" (step-2 demo caption)
→ "Mochi is assembling your team"
```

**Also standardize the title pattern (LOW effort, same fix).** Two pages still carry the retired Hirebase-era tagline:

| Page | Current title | Proposed title |
|---|---|---|
| /blog | "Blog — Kohai — Your Instant AI Workforce" | "Blog — Kohai" |
| /technology | "Technology — Kohai — Your Instant AI Workforce" | "Technology — Kohai" |

"Your Instant AI Workforce" was the May-2026 Hirebase launch framing (01-competitor-analysis.md §5 flags the brand-confusion risk directly). Every page should vote for one entity: Kohai, AI colleagues, Mochi.

**Effort:** string replacements only, no design or layout change. Severity HIGH because the homepage is the entity anchor for every other fix on this list.

## Fix #2 — MEDIUM-HIGH: Comparison money pages carry stale/incorrect competitor pricing

**Problem.** The /vs/* pages are the exact surface 04-content-strategy.md targets for money queries ("Lindy alternative", "Artisan alternative") and the pages AI engines quote when answering "Kohai vs X" — but their competitor numbers are wrong or stale, verified against 01-competitor-analysis.md (vendor pages retrieved 2026-09-08):

- **/vs/lindy** says: "Plans from about $50 to $200 a month… From about $50/month for the assistants you build yourself." Lindy's actual public ladder is **$29.99 / $99.99 / $199.99 per user** — the real entry price is nearly half what our own comparison page claims. Anyone (or any model) cross-checking against lindy.ai/pricing sees an error on the exact page meant to win the "Lindy alternative" searcher. It also accidentally undersells our price advantage: at $29.99/12k credits vs Lindy's $29.99/3k, Kohai ships 4× the credits at the same entry price — the single most quotable fact we have, currently missing.
- **/vs/sintra** says: "about $39/mo for one helper, ~$97/mo for all 12." Current Sintra pricing (01-competitor-analysis.md) is promo-led: **$48.50/mo 1-month, $23.60/mo 3-month, $15.60/mo 12-month** (list $97); individual helper plans are no longer sold. "Last reviewed 17 June 2026" confirms the page predates the current pricing.
- The other /vs/* pages were built from the same template and likely share the vintage — worth a sweep.

Why this matters competitively: 01-competitor-analysis.md documents that **Lindy itself ranks head-to-head pricing takedowns** ("Sintra AI Pricing" on lindy.ai/blog). Fresh, accurate competitor pricing is their SEO weapon; stale numbers on ours hand them the accuracy high ground and give AI engines a reason to cite them instead of us.

**Copy-paste replacements (verified against 01-competitor-analysis.md):**

For /vs/lindy, pricing rows:

```text
"Plans from about $50 to $200 a month, plus a custom plan. Free trial."
→ "Per-user plans: $29.99, $99.99, and $199.99 a month. 7-day free trial; no free tier."

"From about $50/month for the assistants you build yourself."
→ "From $29.99/month per user — 3,000 credits. You build and maintain every assistant yourself."
```

And add the missing favorable fact to the Kohai pricing row:

```text
"From $29.99/month — the whole ready-made team on every plan."
→ "From $29.99/month — 12,000 credits, the whole ready-made team on every plan, with per-agent tool scoping."
```

For /vs/sintra, pricing rows:

```text
"Self-serve: about $39/mo for one helper, ~$97/mo for all 12 (cheaper paid annually)."
→ "Self-serve bundle: $48.50/mo billed monthly, dropping to $15.60–$23.60/mo on 3–12 month commitments (list $97/mo). 250 credits/month."

"From ~$39/month for one helper, ~$97/month for all 12 — drafts only."
→ "From ~$48.50/month (list $97) for all 12 helpers — drafts only, 250 credits/month."
```

Then update each page's "Last reviewed" line to the actual review date, and add a standing note so it doesn't rot again: "Competitor pricing checked [date] against [vendor]/pricing."

**Effort:** copy edits on existing pages. Severity MEDIUM-HIGH rather than HIGH because the pages still rank and convert — but every week of stale numbers is a credibility tax on the money queries.
