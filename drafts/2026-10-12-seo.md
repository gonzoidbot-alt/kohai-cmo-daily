## Fix 1 — Strip the 🐴 emoji from every page `<title>` (severity: HIGH — regression, site-wide)

**Finding:** Every title checked today begins with `🐴 ` — homepage ("🐴 Kōhai | Onboard AI teammates. Get work finished."), /pricing ("🐴 Pricing | Kōhai"), /features, /blog, /vs/marblism, /solutions/solopreneurs, and even the 404 page. This is new since the 10-11 crawl, which recorded clean titles — so it shipped in the last day and regressed every SERP entry at once.

**Why it matters:**
- The emoji plus trailing space consumes the head of every title budget (~2 chars + separator of roughly 560–600 px of pixel width). On tight titles like "🐴 Pricing | Kōhai" it's the single biggest consumer of a title that carries zero query keywords anyway.
- Google frequently rewrites titles it considers low-quality or gimmicky — an emoji-prefixed title on a B2B money page invites the rewrite, and then the page's real title is out of our control.
- It contradicts 03 § voice directly: "one emoji maximum per social post; **often zero**" — that restraint is the brand. An emoji in every SERP listing is the opposite of Japanese-brand restraint, on the one surface where buyers comparison-shop.

**Fix — remove the emoji prefix from the title template** (one template constant, site-wide). While in there, two titles deserve their keyword slot back (the 10-11 draft flagged the homepage one as a bonus; the pricing title has the same gap):

| Page | Current | Candidate |
|---|---|---|
| (template) | `🐴 {page title} \| Kōhai` | `{page title} \| Kōhai` |
| / | `🐴 Kōhai \| Onboard AI teammates. Get work finished.` | `Kōhai — Onboard AI teammates and get work finished \| AI workforce for small business` |
| /pricing | `🐴 Pricing \| Kōhai` | `Kōhai Pricing — AI teammates from $20/month, free to start` |

(Second and third rows use the live /pricing ladder as shown today — $0 free / $20 / $100 / $200. If the ladder changes, the titles change with it.)

## Fix 2 — One live page contradicts the live pricing page (severity: HIGH — consistency, needs Gabe's call)

**Finding:** `/blog/ai-for-small-business` (live today, indexed, and ranking as Kohai's #1–4 result for small-business queries) states **"Kōhai runs $29.99–$200/month"** — while the live /pricing page, the homepage pricing section, and `/blog/best-ai-agents-for-small-business` all state **Free / $20 / $100 / $200**. Google's cached snippet for the blog post even surfaces the $29.99 line ("…from $29.99/month — sales, support, marketing, ops, and more…").

So the drift flagged since 09-27 is no longer docs-vs-site — it is now **site-vs-site**: an indexed evergreen page tells buyers a different entry price than the pricing page they land on next.

**Why it matters:** Mixed price signals across pages are exactly what AI engines refuse to cite, and what a comparison shopper reads as sloppiness or bait-and-switch. It also poisons our own comparison content: the site's "best AI agents" post competes on price clarity against Lindy's takedown posts.

**Fix — two steps, in order:**
1. **Gabe reconciles the ladder first** (standing rule — the discrepancy is noted, not invented around). Whichever of the two ladders is canonical, the decision needs to exist before any copy moves.
2. Then update the one paragraph in `/blog/ai-for-small-business` ("Kōhai runs $29.99–$200/month…" and the closing "from $29.99/month" line) to match the canonical ladder, and sweep the remaining blog posts for `$29.99`/`$99.99`/`$199.99` while in there (the other three posts checked today are clean; the sweep is a site-wide search-and-replace candidate for whatever the old ladder touched).

Drafts referencing $29.99 (quotable-claims list in 06, several 09-month drafts) stay stale until that same reconciliation lands — this fix and that cleanup are one decision.
