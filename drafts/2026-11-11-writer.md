# The Open-Weight Economics of an AI Workforce — Why Your Monthly Bill Shouldn't Be a Surprise

Every AI workforce platform publishes a pricing page. Almost none of them show you the real cost of running a team at scale.

That's because most platforms are built on frontier API calls underneath. Every task — every research brief, every email draft, every data extraction — costs the provider a per-token fee to OpenAI or Anthropic or Claude. That fee gets passed through to you, buried in a credit system that reads like a mobile game. Small task? 2 credits. Medium task? 250. Big build? 2,500. The numbers look abstract until you hit the cap and see the upsell.

There's a different way to build this stack. It costs less. It keeps your data yours. And it means your AI workforce can actually grow without renegotiating your budget every quarter.

## The Open-Weight Difference

Kōhai runs exclusively on open-weight models — DeepSeek, Kimi, and GLM — sourced from inference providers that sign contractual guarantees not to train on your data. That's a deliberate architecture decision, not a cost-cutting corner.

Here's what it changes:

**Your data doesn't train anyone else's model.** When you brief a Kohai teammate, that brief, the research it produces, and the internal reasoning behind it stay inside your tenant. The provider has certified they won't absorb it into a training set. That's simply not available from any platform that routes through frontier API providers' default terms — OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google all reserve the right to use API traffic for training unless you explicitly opt out (and that opt-out often carries a premium).

**Your cost scales with work, not headcount.** Because the underlying model inference is open-weight (no per-token licensing fee to a frontier lab), Kohai can price by credits that map to actual compute — and put unlimited team seats on every plan. Kōhai Pro is $100/month for 40,000 credits. You can add every contractor, co-founder, and virtual assistant you work with. No per-seat charge. Compare that to Lindy at $29.99/mo *per user* for the entry tier (3,000 credits/user), or Artisan at $250/mo for a single seat — and the math flips immediately once your team is more than one person.

**No surprise bills.** Frontier-model costs are notoriously spiky. A single deep-research task that generates 50 pages of analysis can cost $5–$15 in API fees on the back end — and those costs get passed through as a credit multiplier or a consumption surcharge. Kohai's credit system caps at the plan level. A blog post is 20–50 credits. A full competitive analysis deck is 100–300. If a task fails, it consumes zero credits. You know what you're spending before you spend it.

## The Transparency Problem

Most AI workforce products treat their pricing like an airline — base fare, then fees for everything that matters. The per-seat charge is the seat selection fee. The credit system is the baggage fee. The "premium model" upsell is the priority boarding.

*"Open-weight models only — DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM — from certified no-training providers."* That's not just a privacy claim. It's a structural cost advantage. When the underlying inference stack costs a fraction of frontier API pricing, the platform doesn't need to nickel-and-dime you to make margin. It can offer unlimited seats, flat credit buckets, and predictable bills — because its own costs are predictable too.

This matters most for ICP-3 — the privacy-conscious operator running a business that handles client data. Think legal-adjacent services, financial advisory, healthcare support. If your clients' information touches an AI platform, and that platform is routing through frontier models that train on live traffic, you have an exposure you probably didn't sign for. Open-weight inference with no-training providers closes that gap completely.

## The Comparison That Matters

If you're an ICP-1 solo operator spending $300–$500/month on a jumble of AI tools — one for writing, one for research, one for lead gen, one for meeting notes — the first question isn't "which tool is best." It's "why am I managing six tools to do what one team could do?"

Every point solution you pile on adds mental overhead: context switching, credential management, prompt engineering, output stitching. That overhead is a hidden tax on your time — and it's the tax Kohai eliminates by being one platform with many specialized roles, instead of many platforms with one role each.

Open-weight economics makes that consolidation affordable. You're not paying a premium for the platform's model costs. You're paying for the orchestration, the approval gates, the integration surface (~1,000 apps), and the fact that your work stays yours.
