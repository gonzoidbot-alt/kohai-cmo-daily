# What's the Lindy alternative for people who don't want to build workflows?

If you're searching for a Lindy alternative, the reason usually isn't the product's quality — it's the building. Lindy is a capable, well-funded platform with real strengths, and this post credits them honestly. But its model asks you to construct and maintain workflows, and many buyers discover mid-project that construction was the part they were trying to escape.

So the alternative worth evaluating isn't "Lindy but cheaper." It's a different shape entirely: not a platform where you build assistants, but a pre-staffed team of AI colleagues you brief like a hire.

## What is Lindy genuinely good at?

A fair comparison starts with credit where it's due:

- **Slack-native teammates** — Lindy meets you where your team already talks.
- **Approvals built in** — Lindy's own framing is that anything with outside impact waits for approval, which is the right instinct.
- **Breadth** — thousands of integrations, meeting notes, phone agents, computer use.
- **Model choice** — Lindy is model-agnostic, which capability-minded buyers like.

If you read that list and want those things, buy Lindy. The real question is what the model costs you to keep running after the demo.

## Why does building leak time?

The workflow you build on day one is the cheapest it will ever be. Everything after that is upkeep, and the upkeep is yours:

| When | What happens | Who handles it |
|---|---|---|
| Day one | You build the workflow | You |
| Week three | The first edge case breaks the run | You |
| Month two | An upstream app changes its format or permissions | You |
| Every new task | New workflow, new build, new maintenance | You |

None of this is a defect. It's the deal builder platforms offer — power in exchange for upkeep — and for buyers who enjoy the building, it's a good deal. But if your week is already full of sales, content, ops, and support, the upkeep is exactly the category of work you bought software to remove. That gap is what sends people searching for alternatives.

## What does "nothing to build" actually look like?

On Kohai, one message is the whole job description. Write "pull 50 e-commerce leads and draft the outreach," and Mochi, your Chief of Staff, splits the goal and hands each piece to the right colleague — a Sales & Growth Lead, a Marketing & Comms Lead, an Operations Lead — with the tools already connected. Finished work lands in your Library as docs, spreadsheets, decks, and PDFs, not chat replies.

Side by side, the first week looks like this:

| Week one on a builder platform | Week one with a pre-staffed team |
|---|---|
| Map the task into a workflow | Write the outcome as one message |
| Connect and configure each tool | ~1,000 integrations already connected, scoped per colleague |
| Test, debug, and re-run | Review the first finished deliverable in your Library |
| Document it so someone can maintain it | Nothing to document — it's a colleague, not a script |

And the upkeep row disappears with it. Recurring work becomes a schedule — a growth report Monday at 9:00, a pipeline sweep Wednesday at 14:00 — set once, runs weekly. When an action is sensitive, it waits at an approval gate until you say go.

## Where do the two shapes actually differ?

Some differences are smaller than landing pages imply, so it's worth being precise.

**Where they overlap, honestly:** both meter work in credits, both put approval mechanics in front of outside-facing actions, and both offer a trial. Anyone who tells you one category has a monopoly on control is selling something.

**Where they differ structurally:**

- **Seats.** Lindy's tiers are priced per user, so the bill grows with every teammate who touches it. Kohai includes unlimited seats on every plan — your whole team works with the same colleagues at no per-seat charge.
- **Staffing.** A builder platform gives you assistants you configure. Kohai gives you named colleagues who arrive pre-staffed — you brief them, you don't assemble them.
- **Output.** Builders return the results of a run. Kohai returns deliverables — finished files in a Library you can open and review.
- **Upkeep.** Covered above — and it decides most purchases.

**On pricing** (Kohai figures verified 2026-10-19 on kohai.co/pricing): Kōhai Free is $0 with monthly-refreshing credits, no card required. Kōhai is $20/mo with 8,000 credits; Kōhai Pro is $100/mo with 40,000; Kōhai Max is $200/mo with 80,000. Failed tasks consume 0 credits — you pay for finished work, not attempts. For Lindy's current tiers, check lindy.ai directly: their pricing has changed more than once, and this post won't quote numbers it can't keep current. The durable structural note is seats: per-user pricing scales with your team; per-plan pricing doesn't.

## What if privacy is the reason you're leaving?

Some buyers leave a model-agnostic platform for a specific reason: the models. Model-agnostic platforms typically route work through frontier models from the large labs, and if your work touches client data — finance, legal, healthcare-adjacent — what happens to that data in transit matters.

Kohai runs only open-weight models (DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM), sourced from providers certified not to train on your data, inside per-tenant isolation with encryption. Each colleague touches only the tools you scope to it. If your clients ask "does your AI train on our data," the answer is a sentence you can forward without editing.

## How far does "no building" go?

Past the workflow. The deepest version of the question is "what if the task needs software that doesn't exist?" — a small client portal, an internal tracker, a quote calculator. Lindy's ambitions include autonomous building too; the difference is who the building serves. In a builder platform, construction is the product's interface. Here it's something the team does so you don't have to: **"App Studio ships deployed software, not mockups."** You describe the tool; what comes back is built, deployed, and hosted — a running application sitting next to your reports in the Library.

## How do you decide in one week?

1. **List your five recurring jobs** — the report, the chase, the queue, the content, the pipeline.
2. **If you enjoy building,** stay with a builder. An alternative built for people who hate building will feel limiting to you, and that's fine — it wasn't built for you.
3. **If building is the burden,** trial the team: brief two outcomes from different rows of your list on day one.
4. **Score three numbers on both:** time to first finished deliverable, minutes spent setting up, minutes spent maintaining midweek.
5. **Let the maintenance, not the demo, decide.** The demo shows you day one. The bill arrives in week three.

Start with the free plan — no card — and brief one outcome today. If a finished deliverable is in your Library the same day, and nothing along the way needed building, you've answered the question your search query was really asking.
