# A Lindy alternative for founders who don't want to build workflows

Lindy is a good product, and this is not a takedown. If you want to assemble AI automations — connect the nodes, wire the triggers, tune each step — Lindy is one of the serious builder platforms, and its approval features are genuinely well done.

But if you've bounced off it, it's worth being precise about why. Most people who search for a "Lindy alternative" didn't bounce because Lindy's builder is weak. They bounced because building is the job. The alternative that matters isn't a better workshop — it's a different model: colleagues who arrive already hired.

## What is Lindy actually good at?

Plenty. Lindy is Slack-native, so your assistant lives where your team already talks. It offers a deep integration library, computer use, and voice agents. Its approvals are built in — anything with outside impact waits for a yes. And it's model-agnostic, so you can pick frontier models per task. Its pricing ladder runs $29.99 to $199.99 per user per month, with a 7-day free trial.

For a professional who wants a capable assistant inside Slack and enjoys some configuration, that's a strong package. The founder account on X is active, the docs are good, and the company has real funding behind it.

## So where do the two products part ways?

The difference is the unit of work. Lindy sells you a per-user assistant and the tools to build agents yourself. Kohai sells you a pre-staffed team: you describe an outcome, Mochi — your Chief of Staff — splits it into tasks and hands each to the right specialist, and finished work lands in your Library as documents, decks, or spreadsheets.

That's the "AI colleagues, not agents" line, and it's not cosmetic. A builder platform makes you the architect; an ongoing build is a small piece of infrastructure that needs a maintainer. A colleague platform is managed, not maintained — you review the work and re-brief when it misses, the same loop you already run with people.

## What does $29.99 actually buy on each?

The two price ladders are nearly identical, which is exactly why the credits math is worth reading.

| | Lindy Plus | Kohai (entry) |
|---|---|---|
| Price | $29.99/mo per user | $29.99/mo |
| Credits | 3,000 per user | 12,000 |
| Seats | per-user billing | 1 team seat |
| Trial | 7-day free | 7-day free |

Kohai's plan line reads: "First AI hire: $29.99/month, 12,000 credits, 7-day free trial." At the Pro point the pattern holds — $99.99 buys 15,000 credits per user on Lindy and 40,000 credits with 3 team seats on Kohai. Per-dollar, the pre-staffed-team plan carries roughly 3–4× the capacity at the same price points, because it isn't pricing a Slack seat; it's pricing finished work.

Credits aren't the whole story — Marblism's flat hours-included pricing is even simpler to reason about — but between these two, the comparison at equal spend is straightforward.

## What about building apps and sites?

Both products construct software now. Lindy Build assembles apps from a description; Kohai's App Studio does the same, with one checkable difference: "App Studio ships deployed software, not mockups." You get a built, deployed, hosted result — a landing page or internal tool your customers can actually hit — rather than a prototype you then have to ship yourself.

If app-building is a secondary concern for you, this section is a footnote. If it's the reason you're evaluating, it's the section that decides.

## When is Lindy the better choice?

Honestly, in three cases. If your team works in Slack all day and you want the assistant embedded in those threads, Lindy's native surface is something Kohai doesn't replicate. If you want voice agents or a computer-use agent driving a desktop, Lindy ships both. And if you specifically want frontier models rather than open-weight ones, Lindy's model-agnostic setup gives you that choice.

A fair comparison should say that plainly. The right question isn't "which product is better" — it's "which shape fits the operator you are."

## When does the pre-staffed-team model fit better?

When the wiring is the part you keep putting off. If you're a solo operator or a lean team, the honest pattern is: you tried the builder, got two automations half-working, and went back to doing the work yourself. That build → break → fix cycle is a role, and you didn't hire for it.

Kohai's answer is a briefed team with approval gates on anything sensitive — sends, payments, outbound — and per-agent tool scoping, so each colleague only touches what you allow. You describe the outcome once; the recurring version runs on a schedule; you review what lands in the Library.

## How do you decide in one week?

Run the same brief through both. Give each a real task — "draft follow-ups for these five stalled deals," "prep Thursday's investor meeting" — and measure the same two things: how long until finished work exists, and how much of the setup was yours. The product that matches your appetite for building will win that test, whichever one it is. If the answer is "as little as possible," you now know which shape that is.

Kohai's 7-day free trial is long enough to run the comparison on real work, not a demo script.
