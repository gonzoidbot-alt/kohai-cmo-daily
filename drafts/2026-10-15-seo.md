## Fix 1 (HIGH): Close the "AI employee" vocabulary gap in titles, metas, H1s, and body copy

**Severity: High.** This is the single biggest on-page issue. The entire category vocabulary that buyers and SERPs use is "AI employee(s)" / "AI staff" / "AI workers" — today's SERPs for "AI employees for small business" are listicles from Construct, Synchronise, BigDevelop, Workforce AI, Pruta, and others, all ranking on that exact phrase. Lindy's own docs say "Lindy is an AI employee." Kohai deliberately chose "teammates/colleagues" branding — that's a fine product voice, but the site uses it *exclusively*: the phrase "AI employee" appears **zero times** across home, pricing, features, both solution pages, and the /vs pages crawled. Even the blog post at `/blog/what-is-an-ai-employee` (the URL has the keyword!) is titled **"What is an AI teammate?"** — the page's own slug says one thing and its title/H1 say another, throwing away the highest-intent definitional query in the category. Kohai is invisible for the exact head term its ICP types into Google.

The fix keeps the brand voice ("teammate") as primary but puts the category term where crawlers read it. Copy-paste snippets:

**a) Blog post at /blog/what-is-an-ai-employee:**
```html
<title>What Is an AI Employee (and What Does One Actually Do)? | Kōhai Blog</title>
<meta name="description" content="An AI employee has a role, the right tools, memory and accountability. What that means in practice, how it differs from a chatbot, and how AI teammates fit in.">
<h1>What is an AI employee?</h1>
```
(Keep the body's "teammate" voice; use "AI employee" in the H1, first paragraph, and 2–3 H2s naturally.)

**b) Homepage (H1 stays as-is — it's good brand real estate; add the category term to the title + description):**
```html
<title>Kōhai | AI Employees & AI Teammates for Your Business</title>
<meta name="description" content="Onboard AI employees that work like teammates: a fully staffed AI workspace for your business. They finish work across any function; anything irreversible waits for your approval.">
```

**c) /solutions/smb (highest-intent ICP page):**
```html
<title>AI Employees for Small Businesses | Kōhai</title>
<meta name="description" content="Hire AI employees for your small business: pipeline, follow-ups, support triage and reporting handled in the tools you already use. Free to start, no card needed.">
```
(For reference, the current title is the generic "Small businesses | Kōhai".)

**d) /vs pages — add the "alternative" keyword buyers actually search (see also keyword gap below):**
```html
<!-- /vs/lindy, current title: "Kōhai vs. Lindy: which should you choose?" -->
<title>Kōhai vs. Lindy: The AI Employee Alternative to Lindy (2026)</title>
```
Repeat the pattern for /vs/sintra, /vs/marblism, /vs/artisan: "…AI employee alternative to X". Internal links: from /blog/what-is-an-ai-employee link to /solutions/smb and /vs/lindy with anchor text "AI employees for small business" / "Lindy alternative"; from the homepage footer to /vs/ hub ("Compare Kōhai to Lindy, Sintra, Marblism").

**Priority within the fix:** (a) the slug-mismatched blog post is the cheapest, fastest win — the URL already ranks-ready for the term; (b) home title/desc; (c) /solutions/smb; (d) /vs titles.

## Keyword-gap observation: "AI employee(s) for small business" + "Lindy alternative"

**Observation:** Kohai is structurally absent from the two query families its ICP actually types.

1. **"AI employees for small business" / "what is an AI employee" / "AI employee cost."** Today's SERP for the head term is entirely third-party listicles (Construct's "10 platforms compared," Synchronise's "Best AI Employees for Small Business in 2026," BigDevelop's guide) plus category competitors (Workforce AI, Pruta, SUDO) — none of which are Lindy/Artisan/Sintra, i.e., the listicle sites are winning *Kohai's* buyers by default. Kohai owns the perfect assets for these queries (a dedicated definitional post, an SMB solutions page, a pricing page with real cost explanations like "What an AI job actually costs") but every asset is titled in "teammate" vocabulary, so nothing can rank for the term buyers use. Fix 1 addresses the on-page side; the content side needs one honest "AI employees for small business: platforms compared (2026)" pillar post that includes Kōhai among the options — same play that works for everyone currently on page one.

2. **"Lindy AI alternative" / "alternatives to Lindy" (and the same pattern for Sintra/Marblism).** This query family is a feeding frenzy: Gumloop, Vellum, Taskade, alfred_, Odella, and Lindy's own "Relevance AI alternatives" page all run listicles targeting it, because competitor-alternative searches are the highest-intent traffic in this category (Lindy's ladder is nearly price-identical to Kōhai's, so Lindy's searchers are qualified Kōhai buyers). Kohai already has the right pages — /vs/lindy, /vs/sintra, /vs/marblism — but their titles ("Kōhai vs. Lindy: which should you choose?") don't contain the word "alternative," so they can't surface for the phrasing buyers use, and no third-party listicle includes Kōhai yet. Two moves: (a) retitle the /vs pages per Fix 1(d); (b) get Kōhai listed in the third-party "Lindy alternatives" roundtables via the outreach/affiliate workstream — Sintra's affiliate program and Lindy's competitive-SEO playbook (01-competitor-analysis.md §"Where Kohai is vulnerable") both show this channel compounds.

**Prioritization logic:** family 1 is volume + ICP-fit and is fixable mostly on-site this week; family 2 is smaller volume but higher purchase intent and needs off-site distribution. Do both, starting with Fix 1 which serves them simultaneously.
