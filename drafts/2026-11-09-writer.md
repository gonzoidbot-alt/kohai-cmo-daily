# Lindy vs Artisan vs Kohai: a factual comparison for the founder who wants work done

You've seen the headlines. AI employees, AI agents, AI colleagues — the category has a dozen names and every platform claims to run your business. But when you actually sit down to compare, the feature tables blur together. Every product says it connects to your tools. Every product says it's the cheapest. Every product says it replaces a human hire.

What doesn't blur: what it actually costs you when a platform's economic model breaks.

Here's a three-way comparison of Lindy, Artisan, and Kohai — not on feature checkboxes, but on what each platform's design means for the operator who has to get work done this week. The numbers are public and verified from each company's site as of October 2026.

## The surface-level differences

### Lindy — the builder's AI

Lindy is the most established name in AI agents-as-a-service. Its credit system is granular: everyday tasks cost 2–250 credits, deep work costs 250–1,000, and big builds run 1,000–2,500. If you enjoy configuring workflows and tweaking prompts, Lindy gives you the most knobs.

But those knobs come with a cost — literally. Lindy's per-user pricing means a team of three on the Pro plan pays $299.97/month. Failed tasks still consume credits. And credits expire monthly, so unused capacity at the end of the month is gone.

### Artisan — the sales-first AI employee

Artisan positions its AI employees as full-time hires with names like Ava (Sales) and Blake (Recruiting). Its pricing leans enterprise: the Intern plan runs $250/month annual for 12k credits, and the Employee plan runs $600/month annual for 30k credits with Salesforce sync and priority support. Artisan includes white-glove setup — a CSM from day one — which is valuable for teams that need hand-holding but adds overhead for the solo operator who just wants to brief and go.

Artisan's credit model is Action-based rather than task-estimated. The free tier (300 credits/mo) works for evaluation, but any serious usage jumps to $250/mo — a steeper leap than Lindy's or Kohai's free-to-paid transition.

### Kohai — the colleague you brief, not build

Kohai's design starts from a different premise: you brief a colleague, not configure an agent. One message is the whole job description — no workflow diagram, no prompt template, no builder canvas. The result lands in your Library, and sensitive actions stop at an approval gate until you say go.

Its credit system is built for predictability. A blog post costs 20–50 credits, a deck costs 100–300. Failed tasks consume zero credits. Add-on credits never expire. And every plan includes unlimited seats, so adding your Strategy Lead, Sales Lead, and Marketing Lead to the same task doesn't multiply the bill.

## The real differentiator: cost of unpredictability

Feature comparisons are useful. But the question that matters more: **what happens when the platform's economic model surprises you?**

With Lindy, the surprise is per-user pricing plus monthly-expiring credits. A growing team that hires their third member sees a 50% cost increase. A big project that needs more compute runs into tier boundaries. Credits you paid for but didn't use vanish at month-end.

With Artisan, the surprise is the jump from free to paid — 300 credits to 12,000 is a real capability step, but $250/mo is a commitment the solo operator wasn't ready for. Annual contracts and cancellation friction are documented patterns (third-party audits flag auto-renewal friction).

With Kohai, the predictability is structural: one price for the whole team, credits that don't expire, failed tasks that cost nothing, and ~1,000 integrations connected day one, with per-agent tool scoping. You know what this month costs before it starts.

## The edge cases that break each model

**The solo founder growing from 1 to 3:** Lindy's bill triples. Artisan's bill jumps to $250. Kohai's stays the same (unlimited seats).

**The agency with 5 clients:** Lindy needs 5 paid seats. Artisan's $600 Employee plan covers one. Kohai's Pro plan covers all of them.

**The operator who doesn't know credit math:** Lindy's 2–1,000 credit range per task is hard to estimate. Artisan's Action model is opaque. Kohai publishes per-deliverable estimates (blog post: 20–50, deck: 100–300) so you can see what work costs before you assign it.

**The privacy-conscious business:** Lindy supports all models including frontier providers that train on data. Artisan uses proprietary + frontier. Kohai runs exclusively on open-weight models from certified no-training providers, with per-tenant isolation and encryption.

## Which one should you start with?

If you enjoy building workflows and want the most flexible agent canvas, Lindy is a capable tool — just budget for per-user scaling.

If you want white-glove onboarding and sales automation specifically, Artisan's named employees and CSM model work well for teams that need setup hand-holding.

If you want a colleague — not a project — who starts working after one brief, costs a predictable amount, and handles the sensitive parts through approval gates, Kohai is structured for exactly that.

All three will get work done. Only one makes your bill predictable, your credits non-expiring, and your whole team included at one price.
