# Your Monday Operations Workflow, Fully Automated

You wake up on Monday to 47 unread emails, a Slack channel that didn't sleep, a support ticket backlog, and the creeping feeling that you're already behind before coffee. By Friday, you'll have answered the same three questions six different ways, manually exported a spreadsheet that was stale the moment you opened it, and postponed the strategic work yet again.

This is the solo founder's week — and it's not a capacity problem. It's a delegation problem. You don't need more hours; you need a way to hand off the repetitive operations work without hiring a full-time ops coordinator at $50K+ a year.

Here's how to delegate ops to an AI team — starting with your Monday morning.

## The Monday report, written before you sit down

Every founder needs a pulse check to start the week: revenue last week, open invoices, support volume, pipeline changes, active projects. Most founders spend 30-60 minutes Monday morning pulling this from Stripe, the CRM, the ticketing tool, and Google Analytics, then formatting it into something readable.

**What it looks like with a Kōhai Operations Lead:**

You send one brief: "Monday ops report — pull revenue from Stripe, open pipeline from the CRM, ticket volume and aging from support, and highlight anything from last week that needs a decision from me."

That's the whole instruction. The Operations Lead connects to your tools through the ~1,000 integrations available, pulls the data, formats it into a clean summary, and lands it in your Library. No dashboards to configure. No SQL queries. No Zapier chains to debug.

Time cost: 30 seconds to write the brief vs. 45 minutes to pull and format the report. That's roughly 3.5 hours back every month from one recurring task.

## Support triage: first response in seconds, not hours

Customer support is the black hole of the solo operator's week. Every interruption costs 15-25 minutes of context-switching recovery. By midweek, you're either ignoring tickets to get work done or answering tickets and never doing the work.

**The delegated version:**

Your Customer Support Lead gets a brief describing your support policies, refund thresholds, escalation triggers, and common response templates. From there, it triages incoming tickets autonomously:

- **Known issues** get answered with a relevant solution from your knowledge base.
- **Billing questions** get the pricing info or refund policy applied per your rules.
- **Escalation triggers** (refund above threshold, angry customer, technical bug) route to an approval gate — you get a summary and approve the response before it sends.
- **Everything else** gets a first response within 60 seconds, not 6 hours.

The approval gate is the important part. Sensitive actions pause until you say go. You stay in control without being in the middle of every conversation.

## Invoice follow-up: the collection you'd rather outsource

Nobody enjoys chasing invoices. But for service businesses, delayed payments mean cash flow gaps. The typical solo founder sends 2-3 reminder sequences per week, manually tracking who's been pinged, who promised to pay, and who needs the third escalation.

**The delegated version:**

Your Sales & Growth Lead gets a brief: "Review open invoices over 15 days past due. Send a polite first reminder. If unpaid at 30 days, escalate with the late-fee terms. Flag any invoices over $2K at 45 days for my review."

The lead checks your billing platform, identifies overdue invoices, sends the appropriate sequence, and only surfaces the exceptions. You review and approve the escalation batch (or not) — the rest runs without your attention.

Time saved: roughly 90 minutes per week that used to go into awkward payment nudges.

## End-of-day sync without the download

By 5 PM you should know what got done, what's pending, and what needs your decision tomorrow. Most founders end the day with a fuzzy feeling of unfinished work and no clear picture of what actually moved.

**The delegated version:**

Your Strategy Lead generates a daily closeout: deliverables shipped today, workstreams completed, items waiting on you, and tomorrow's priority list. It lands in your Library automatically. You scan it in 60 seconds, approve or redirect, then close the day with a real handoff — not a gnawing sense of things falling through cracks.
