# Your Monday Morning, Reclaimed: One Operator's Playbook for Delegating Ops to AI

Monday morning hits and you've already lost four hours. Growth report due. Pipeline needs a sweep. Three invoices are past due and nobody's chased them. Support tickets stacked up over the weekend. And someone — probably you — needs to prep the agenda for the 10 a.m. standup.

This is the story of every solo founder's week. The real work — the building, the strategy, the revenue-generating work — waits until the operational noise is cleared. For most founders, that never happens.

Here's the alternative. One afternoon setting up an AI teammate to own the Monday morning ops loop. No agent-builder, no workflow diagram, no prompt engineering course. Just a brief, a few approvals, and a week that starts at 9 a.m. instead of 6 a.m.

## What does "delegating ops" actually look like?

Let's get specific. A solo operator's Monday-morning ops stack usually includes:

- A revenue dashboard to pull (Stripe, maybe a spreadsheet export)
- A pipeline review (CRM check — who stalled, who needs a nudge)
- Invoice follow-ups on overdue accounts
- Support ticket triage (urgent vs. nice-to-have)
- A meeting prep brief for any calls this week
- A content post to schedule (LinkedIn or X, however consistent)

That's six separate tasks. Most involve logging into different tools, running the same queries you ran last week, and formatting the answers. None of them is hard. Together, they eat half a day.

Now try this instead: you send one message to your Operations Lead. Something like:

> _"Monday ops roundup. Pull last week's revenue from Stripe, flag any deals that haven't moved in 10 days, chase the three overdue invoices, triage support tickets by urgency, and draft a meeting brief for Tuesday's investor call. Deliverables in my Library by 10 a.m."_

That's the brief. Not a prompt chain, not a workflow JSON, not a configuration menu. A brief — exactly what you'd say to a competent hire on their first day.

## What happens next

Your Operations Lead spins up the relevant teammates — the Sales & Growth Lead handles the pipeline sweep, the Customer Support Lead triages tickets, the Strategy Lead pulls the revenue snapshot and formats the report. Each one works in the tools they're scoped to. Sensitive actions (like sending an invoice chase or replying to a ticket) hit an approval gate — you review, approve, or edit before anything goes out.

By 10 a.m., you have:

1. A revenue summary with week-over-week comparison
2. A pipeline report highlighting three stalled deals
3. Draft invoice reminders for the three overdue accounts (waiting at the approval gate)
4. Support tickets sorted: 2 urgent, 4 routine, 1 can wait
5. A meeting brief with investor background and talking points
6. A draft LinkedIn post queued for review

Six deliverables. One message. And your hands never touched a tool.

## Why this beats an agent-builder

The difference is not subtle. Agent-building platforms (Lindy, Relevance AI, n8n) let you construct workflows — you design the pipeline, wire the triggers, test the logic, and maintain it when something breaks. That's fine if you enjoy building tools. It's a tax if you're trying to run a business.

Kōhai works the other way: describe the outcome, and the right colleagues assemble to produce it. You don't manage the architecture. You brief it like a person.

This matters most for the **recurring, never-done work** — the Monday report, the invoice chase, the weekly content post. In an agent-builder, you'd reconstruct the workflow from scratch or maintain a complex automation. In Kōhai, you brief once, approve the result, and the next iteration learns from what you approved. The pattern reinforces itself.

## The one-time setup that makes it work

The first Monday takes the longest because you're setting expectations. Here's what that looks like:

1. **Connect your tools.** Kōhai integrates with roughly 1,000 apps — Stripe, Gmail, Notion, Slack, HubSpot, the usual stack. One auth flow per tool, done in about 15 minutes.
2. **Name your colleagues.** The five default leads (Strategy, Sales & Growth, Marketing & Comms, Customer Support, Operations) are already named and scoped. You can add custom roles later.
3. **Brief the first task.** Send the Monday ops brief above. The first deliverable lands in your Library within an hour. Review it, approve or request edits, and the pattern is set.
4. **Set the approval gates.** Decide which actions need your say-so before they execute. Outgoing emails? Approve first. Internal reports? Ship freely. You set the threshold per colleague.

That's it. No workflow builder, no API keys to configure, no "training" the model on your business. The colleagues arrive knowing how to use the tools you connected.

## When you should pass on this approach

This playbook works best when you know what you want done and want it delivered without managing the machinery. It's not the right call if:

- You want to build and sell agent workflows as a product (Kōhai is a user, not a platform)
- You enjoy prompt engineering as a craft — Kōhai removes the thing you enjoy
- Your org runs at enterprise-automation scale with dedicated platform teams

For everyone else — the solo founder who needs the Monday morning back, the lean team that needs every function staffed without adding headcount, the operator who wants to approve, not build — this is the fastest path to reclaimed hours.
