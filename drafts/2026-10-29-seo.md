## Fix 1 (HIGH severity): Money pages target brand language, not the digest's money keywords

**The problem.** The digest's SEO/GEO targets are: "AI employees for small business", "AI workforce platform", "hire AI employees", "Lindy alternative", "Artisan alternative". Zero of these phrases appear in any crawled title, meta description, or H1. The site's own brand register ("AI teammates", "teammates") is everywhere instead — searchers and AI engines don't query in brand register. Specifically:

| Page | Current title | Money keyword it should carry |
|---|---|---|
| `/solutions/smb` | "Small businesses \| Kōhai" | "AI employees for small business" |
| Homepage | "Onboard AI teammates. Get work finished." | "AI workforce platform" / "hire AI employees" |
| `/solutions/solopreneurs` | "Solopreneurs \| Kōhai" | "AI employees for solopreneurs / solo founders" |
| `/vs/lindy` | "Kōhai vs. Lindy: which should you choose?" | "Lindy alternative" (see keyword gap below) |

`/solutions/smb` is the worst offender: exact-intent page, generic title, no descriptive meta at all (crawled live HTML has none).

**Copy-paste snippets:**

`/solutions/smb` `<head>`:
```html
<title>AI Employees for Small Business | Kōhai</title>
<meta name="description" content="Hire AI employees for your small business: a Chief of Staff plus sales, marketing, support and ops leads, working in your existing tools. From $0 to start, unlimited seats on every plan.">
```
First H2 on that page (repeat the phrase once in body copy):
```html
<h2>What AI employees do for a small business</h2>
```

Homepage title + meta (keep the brand hook, add the category phrase):
```html
<title>Kōhai — Hire AI Employees: the AI Workforce Platform for Small Business</title>
<meta name="description" content="Kōhai is the AI workforce platform where you hire AI employees, not build agents. A full team of AI colleagues works in your tools, and you approve anything irreversible. Start free.">
```
(The H1 can stay "Onboard AI teammates. Get work finished." — one keyword landing in title+meta is enough; the H1 is doing brand work.)

**Why it's ranked #1:** these are the digest's declared money keywords, the pages already exist (no build cost — a `<head>` edit per page), and the homepage's title is currently 100% brand-register with zero category coverage. Everything downstream (rankings, AI-engine citations) is capped until the pages say what they are.

## Fix 2 (MEDIUM severity): robots.txt blocks Gemini grounding — self-sabotage against the GEO strategy

**The problem.** The GTM playbook (06 § Stage 1) makes "being quotable by models" a named job, with a monthly 10-prompt AI visibility audit. But robots.txt `Disallow: /` for **Google-Extended** and **Google-CloudVertexBot** blocks Gemini from retrieving kohai.co content for grounding — Gemini literally cannot quote a site it can't fetch. That's a direct conflict with the GEO pillar, and it's almost certainly a Cloudflare managed-presets accident, not a deliberate choice: the site's own Content-Signal line says `ai-train=no, use=reference` (reference use is *permitted*), and PerplexityBot / OAI-SearchBot / ChatGPT-User are already allowed.

Keep the blocks that match the brand: GPTBot, ClaudeBot, CCBot, Bytespider, Applebot-Extended, Amazonbot are training crawlers, and `ai-train=no` is a genuine Kohai positioning pillar (open-weight, no-training). Those stay.

**Copy-paste snippet — in the Cloudflare robots.txt managed settings, remove these two blocks:**
```
# REMOVE (grounding/retrieval bots, not training bots):
User-agent: Google-Extended      # blocks Gemini grounding/AI answers
Disallow: /
User-agent: Google-CloudVertexBot # blocks Vertex AI enterprise grounding
Disallow: /
```
Resulting state: training crawlers blocked (privacy pillar intact), grounding/search crawlers allowed (GEO pillar unblocked).

**Why it's #2:** likely-zero cost (two toggles in Cloudflare), but its impact is gated behind Fix 1 — unblocking Gemini only pays off once the pages carry quotable, keyword-matched claims. Hence second priority.

## Keyword-gap observation: "Lindy alternative" has no page — only "Kōhai vs. Lindy"

The digest lists "Lindy alternative" as a money keyword, and the 06 audit prompts include *"Lindy alternative that doesn't require building workflows."* The site's `/vs/lindy` page targets comparison intent ("Kōhai vs. Lindy: which should you choose?") — but almost nobody types "Kōhai vs. Lindy" into a search box; they type "Lindy alternative" (the competitor's own name, high volume, late-stage intent). The current title, H1, and meta contain neither the word "alternative" nor "Lindy" in an alternative-intent construction. Same pattern on `/vs/artisan` ("Artisan alternative" is the other digest money keyword).

Suggested title/H1 rewrite for `/vs/lindy` (no new page needed):
```html
<title>The Lindy Alternative That Needs No Workflow Building | Kōhai</title>
<meta name="description" content="Looking for a Lindy alternative? Kōhai gives you a whole team of AI employees on one plan — nothing to build, unlimited seats, from $20/month with a $0 start.">
```
Body addition (first paragraph, per the 06 playbook's "question as H2, answer in first 2 sentences"):
```html
<h2>Why look for a Lindy alternative?</h2>
<p>Lindy prices per person per seat and asks you to build agent workflows.
Kōhai is the alternative with nothing to build: a full staffed team of AI
employees on one plan, from $20/month.</p>
```
Longer term, an `/vs/` hub page ("Best AI employees: Kohai vs. every alternative" — buyer-guide format per 06 § Stage 2) would aggregate the 16 individual pages' internal-link equity. Noted as follow-up, not a today fix.
