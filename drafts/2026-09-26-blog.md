# AI employees for small business: hire the work, not the toolkit

If you run a business with five people or fewer, you've met the agent-builder pitch by now: connect some nodes, describe a workflow, wire a trigger, and automation appears. What the pitch leaves out is who keeps the automation alive. Every built agent is a small piece of infrastructure, and infrastructure needs a maintainer — a role your five-person company hasn't got.

So the useful question isn't "which agent should I build?" It's "who should I hire?" An AI employee is the hiring answer: a named teammate you brief the way you'd brief a new hire, working in the tools you already use, delivering finished work you review.

## What is an AI employee?

An AI employee is a colleague with a role — a Chief of Staff, a Sales & Growth Lead, an Operations Lead — that you brief in plain language and that produces deliverables, not chat replies. You describe the outcome once. The work gets done, and the results land somewhere you can find them again.

The contrast is with an AI agent, which is software you assemble and then own. Agents are powerful, but ownership means maintenance: when a connected tool changes its API or a step misfires, the fix is yours. A colleague is managed, not maintained. You review the work and re-brief when it misses — the same loop you already run with people.

## Why hiring fits a team with no engineers

Building agents is a craft, and crafts take time: time to learn the builder, time to construct each workflow, and time to repair things when they break. A company with an engineering team can absorb that. A two-person company can't, because the person doing the building is also the person doing sales, support, and delivery.

There's also the compounding problem. Every workflow you build becomes a permanent possession — a private software estate that only its author understands. Take a week off and the estate waits. Hiring avoids the estate entirely: the machinery belongs to the platform, and your job stays the part you're actually good at, which is deciding what "done" looks like.

| | Build agents yourself | Hire AI employees |
|---|---|---|
| Time to first useful work | Days to weeks of setup | Same day — first deliverable in your Library |
| When a connected tool changes | You debug it | The platform handles it |
| Adding a new responsibility | Build another workflow | Brief another colleague |
| Who owns the machinery | You do, permanently | The platform does |
| Skill required | Systems building | Managing people — which you already have |

## Day one is onboarding, not installation

The real test of an AI hire is what happens the day you brief it. On Kohai, the team arrives with its tools already connected — "~1,000 integrations connected day one, with per-agent tool scoping." Your Operations Lead can reach the project tracker without reaching your bank account. Access is granted by role, deliberately, and revocable at any time.

That's the difference between a hire and a toolkit. A toolkit starts its first day asking you to configure it. A hire starts its first day working, inside boundaries you set — the same way you'd hand a new employee an email account and a scope of responsibility, and nothing else.

## What your first hire costs

The entry math is small enough to test without a meeting: "First AI hire: $29.99/month, 12,000 credits, 7-day free trial."

Credits price the work, not the machinery. A blog post runs 20–50 credits, a deck 100–300, so you can see what a task costs before you assign it — and the plan price caps the month before it starts. Against the alternatives, the math stops being close: a human hire at several thousand a month, or your own evenings spent building and babysitting workflows.

## When building is the right call

The honest exception, stated plainly: some founders should build. If automation is your product — you're a consultant shipping workflows, or you genuinely enjoy the builder's canvas — then build. Lindy is a serious builder platform whose approval features are genuinely well done, and Relevance AI is a capable platform for teams that want to construct custom workforces with their own logic. Both are made by people who take that craft seriously.

Kohai isn't for the person who loves the machinery. It's for the founder who tried the machinery, counted the maintenance hours, and realized what they wanted was an employee — briefed once, reviewed after, never debugged.

## Make your first hire this week

Pick the recurring task that eats your Monday — the growth report, the invoice chase, the support inbox — and brief it the way you'd brief a capable person on their first day: the outcome, the tone, the decisions that stay yours. The 7-day free trial covers the whole experiment, and the first deliverable is usually in your Library before dinner.

You're not adopting a technology. You're making a hire. It just starts faster than any hire you've made before.
