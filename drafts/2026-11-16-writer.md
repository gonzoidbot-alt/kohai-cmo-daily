# What an AI Hire Actually Costs: Price It Per Reclaimed Hour

You can compare AI workforce platforms by sticker price. Most people do. It's the wrong number.

Sticker price tells you what you pay. It doesn't tell you what you get back, and the two are not proportional. A $200 plan is not "twice the hire" of a $100 plan if the hours it returns to you don't double. A free trial that produces nothing is expensive at $0.

The number that actually settles the question is smaller than any tier: dollars per reclaimed hour. Here's how to compute it, using Kohai's own published example week as the worked case.

## The cost side: what a month buys

Kohai's pricing is credit-based. Four tiers, current as of this writing:

| Plan | Price | Monthly credits |
|---|---|---|
| Kōhai Free | $0 (limited time, no card) | refreshes monthly |
| Kōhai | $20/mo | 8,000 |
| Kōhai Pro | $100/mo | 40,000 |
| Kōhai Max | $200/mo | 80,000 |

Every plan includes unlimited seats and every role template. Credits are the only meter.

What a credit buys, by task size:

- Small tasks (a lookup, a short draft, a status check): 5–10 credits
- Medium tasks (a chart, a landing page, a blog post): 20–50 credits
- Large tasks (a deck, a research brief): 100–300 credits

Two rules that matter more than the table. Failed tasks consume 0 credits — you don't pay for work that didn't ship. And add-on credits never expire — you don't pay twice for a slow month.

Do the arithmetic on the $20 plan: 8,000 credits ÷ ~4.3 weeks ≈ 1,850 credits a week. Against the task sizes above, that's a working week that mixes dozens of small tasks with a handful of medium ones and a large deliverable or two. That's the supply side. Now the demand side.

## The value side: what a week gives back

**Kōhai's example week: 24 deliverables shipped, 31 hours reclaimed.** That's the platform's published illustration of a working week — an example, not a promise. Treat it the way you'd treat any sample: as a worked case, not a guarantee. Then run your own number against it.

If your week looked like that example week, the math on the $20 plan is:

- $20 per month ÷ 31 reclaimed hours ≈ **$0.65 per reclaimed hour**
- 8,000 credits ÷ 24 deliverables ≈ **77 credits per deliverable** on average — a blend of small, medium, and large work

Hold the $0.65 up against your own hourly rate. If you're a solo operator billing clients at $75 or $150 an hour, every reclaimed hour you reinvest in billable work returns 100–200× what it costs. If you reinvest it in sales calls instead of invoice chasing, the return is whatever your close rate makes it. You don't need a benchmark report for any of this — you need your own rate and one honest week of tracking.

Notice what's missing from that comparison: nothing in it depends on which model runs the work, how many seats you bought, or how impressive the demo was. Reclaimed hours are the only denominator that matters, and they're measurable in week one.

## The risk row most comparisons skip

Every cost model has a downside scenario. For an AI hire, it's "I paid and the work wasn't usable." Ask one question of any platform: what happens when the work fails?

On Kohai, failed tasks consume 0 credits. The retry doesn't cost you a second time. That's not a rounding error in the math — it changes the shape of the risk. Per-task pricing with no charge for failures means your downside per task is time, not money. Per-seat pricing with charge-anyway consumption means your downside is both.

You can verify this one in a trial week: look at the credit ledger after a task fails. If the number didn't move, the pricing behaves the way it's described.

## Why the per-credit price can stay this low

One structural reason the $20 tier exists at all: Kohai runs exclusively on open-weight models — DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM — from inference providers certified not to train on your data. Open weights mean the platform doesn't pay a frontier-model premium on every token, and that saving passes through to the per-credit price instead of into a moat.

For you, that shows up in two places in the math. First, the sticker: $20 for a full working week's supply of credits is possible because the underlying inference isn't the expensive part. Second, your data: the same certified no-training providers that keep costs down also keep your client material out of anyone's training set. The economics and the privacy are the same decision, not two.

## The formula to run before you subscribe

Whatever platform you're evaluating, the math is three lines:

1. **Monthly cost** — the plan you'd actually pick, not the one the page highlights.
2. **Reclaimed hours** — hours of your week the work takes off your plate. Track one real week; estimates lie.
3. **Divide.** If cost-per-reclaimed-hour is a rounding error against your own rate, the decision is made. If it isn't, no feature table will save it.

If you want a worked example to check the method against, the numbers above are public: a $20 plan, an example week of 24 deliverables and 31 reclaimed hours, failed tasks at zero. Kōhai Free is running at $0 for a limited time — no card — so you can price your own week before you pay anything. That's the whole evaluation: run one week, count the hours, divide.
