# How to staff a solo founder's week with an AI team

Run the math on your own calendar first. A solo founder's week is mostly recurring work that someone else could do: the Monday report, the pipeline sweep, the invoice chase, the support queue, the content slot nobody gets to. The work is necessary, repetitive, and never done — and it's why the week is full before the real work starts.

You don't fix this with one clever tool. You fix it the way you'd fix it with people: you staff the week. The difference is that with an AI team, the hires cost credits instead of salary, and the first one starts today.

## What does a solo founder actually delegate?

Four kinds of work eat the calendar, and each maps to a colleague with a named role:

| Workstream | What it eats | Who takes it |
|---|---|---|
| Reporting & ops | The Monday report, status updates, invoice chases | Operations Lead |
| Sales outreach | Lead lists, sequences, replies | Sales & Growth Lead |
| Content & SEO | Posts, emails, landing copy | Marketing & Comms Lead |
| Support triage | The ticket queue, after hours included | Customer Support Lead |

A point-solution tool fixes one column of this table. So you buy seven, and now part of the week is managing the tools. That's the trap the tool-sprawl moment comes from: leverage that needs a manager isn't leverage. A colleague with a role is different — the work comes back finished, and nobody on your side maintained anything to get it.

Mochi, your Chief of Staff, sits above all four. You brief Mochi in one message; Mochi splits the goal into tasks and hands each to the right specialist with the right tools already connected.

## What does the staffed week look like?

Here's the pattern, day by day. Each brief states the outcome and the check — nothing else.

| Day | The brief | What lands in your Library |
|---|---|---|
| Mon 9:00 | "Growth report: pipeline changes, support volume, cash position, one page" | The report, finished, before your first meeting |
| Wed 14:00 | "Pipeline sweep: flag stale deals, draft the follow-ups" | Updated pipeline + follow-up drafts |
| Fri 16:00 | "Chase the three overdue invoices" | Drafted chases, **held at the approval gate** |
| Daily | "Triage new support tickets; draft replies, don't send" | Triage summary + reply drafts |

Notice what the week doesn't include: you reviewing prompts, fixing workflow diagrams, or checking whether the tool connected. The recurring work runs on schedules you set once. Sensitive actions — money, outbound sends, anything with outside impact — wait at an approval gate until you say go.

The gate is doing more work than it looks like. It's the reason you can hand over an invoice chase without holding your breath: the colleague does the drafting, and the send is yours to approve. Control isn't a setting you hunt for later — it's the default shape of the workflow.

Start with one day, not five. The Monday report alone reclaims the hour you'd have spent assembling it, and it teaches you how you brief. Add Wednesday when the first deliverable reads like something you'd have written. By Friday, the pattern is yours.

## Won't setup eat the time you saved?

This is where most AI tooling loses the week it promised to save. Setup is the tax: connect the apps, map the fields, maintain the glue. A workforce you have to assemble isn't a workforce, it's a project.

Kohai's answer is on the label: ~1,000 integrations connected day one, with per-agent tool scoping. Day one means the connections exist before your first brief — you don't spend week one wiring Gmail to Sheets. Per-agent scoping means each hire touches only what you allow: your Sales & Growth Lead sees the CRM, not your books. The tools question stops being a setup task and becomes a permission you set once.

## What does a staffed week cost?

Credits, not tokens — and the price of a task is published before you assign it. A blog post runs 20–50 credits. A deck runs 100–300. You can price the week before you brief it, which is the part usage-based AI billing never let you do.

Compare that to the alternative accounting: a point-solution subscription per tool, plus the hours you spend operating them. One is a line item you chose. The other is a stack you defend.

That predictability is the point. Your AI bill shouldn't be a surprise, and a first hire shouldn't require a procurement process. There's a 7-day free trial, and the first deliverable lands in your Library the same day — you'll know if the pattern works before you pay anything.

## Your first week, in order

1. **Pick the one recurring task you dread most.** For most founders it's the Monday report. Write its brief in three sentences: outcome, audience, check.
2. **Set the schedule.** Mon 9:00. Set once, runs weekly.
3. **Read the first deliverable like an editor, not a fixer.** Correct in a sentence if needed — the brief improves, not the prompt.
4. **Add a second day** only after the first deliverable reads like yours.

The compounding part isn't the hours — though Kohai's published example week shows 31 reclaimed. It's that the week stops filling itself before your real work starts. Staff it once, and every Monday after is yours.
