# The four shapes of AI employee platform (and which one fits your team)

You've probably noticed the products in this category don't feel comparable. One wants you to build workflows, one gives you twelve helpers, one is built for outbound sales, one hands you a finished team. That's not confusion on your part — the "AI employee" label covers at least four different products.

Buying the wrong shape wastes a trial, not just a month. This post sorts the category into its four shapes, credits what each does well, and gives you a one-week test for the shortlist.

## Why doesn't one product fit everyone?

Because the products answer different jobs. Relevance AI is a builder platform — you compose your own agents and the logic between them. Lindy is a workflow builder with a per-user assistant experience on top. Artisan focuses on one job — outbound sales — and goes deep on it. Sintra sells a roster of twelve named helpers on a credit meter. Marblism and Kohai hand you a staffed team and put the maintenance on their side.

None of these is the wrong product. Each is the right product for a different buyer, and the fastest way to a good decision is to name your shape first.

## The category, sorted by shape

| Shape | Named examples | You do | They do | Typical pricing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Builder platform | Relevance AI | Compose agents and logic | Provide the tooling | Platform tiers |
| Workflow builder | Lindy | Wire triggers and steps | Host and run them | $29.99–$199.99 per user/mo |
| Vertical BDR | Artisan AI | Run outbound with a specialist | Own the whole sales motion | Sales-tier pricing |
| Helper roster | Sintra | Pick a helper per task | Staff 12 named roles | ~250 credits, discount-led |
| Pre-staffed team | Kohai, Marblism | Brief outcomes | Staff, run, and maintain the roles | Kohai $29.99 flat; Marblism $24–39 flat |

The rows aren't ranked — a builder platform is genuinely the right answer if composing agents is the work you enjoy. The rows differ in one thing: where the building happens. Three shapes put it on your side of the table; the last two put it on theirs.

## Which shape fits which buyer?

| If you... | Your shape | Honest trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Enjoy composing automations and want full control | Builder platform (Relevance AI) | You own design and maintenance |
| Live in Slack and want a configurable per-user assistant | Workflow builder (Lindy) | Setup time is yours; per-user billing scales with headcount |
| Need one job done — outbound — done deeply | Vertical BDR (Artisan) | Narrow scope by design |
| Want many small named helpers at a low entry price | Helper roster (Sintra) | Credit pool is small; watch the discount-led math at renewal |
| Want finished deliverables you review, not tooling you run | Pre-staffed team (Kohai, Marblism) | Less hands-on control than a builder |

That last row is the whole distinction between the camps. Builder shapes sell you capability; staffed shapes sell you outcomes. Neither is a scam — but a buyer who wants outcomes will be frustrated by capability, and vice versa.

## What about pricing and predictability?

Two pricing patterns exist, and they reward different habits. Per-user ladders (Lindy's $29.99/$99.99/$199.99) and credit meters (Sintra's ~250-credit pool) scale with usage and seats; flat plans (Kohai $29.99, Marblism $24–39) scale with nothing. If your volume varies month to month, flat is easier to budget; if it's steady and you want headroom, a ladder can win.

Kohai's entry line: "First AI hire: $29.99/month, 12,000 credits, 7-day free trial." The credits are the working capacity — at 12,000, the first month's deliverables are covered without a second decision, and the trial covers the evaluation itself. Marblism's flat 50 work-hours per month is the closest comparable promise in the staffed camp; compare the two on what a "work-hour" or a "credit" actually buys in finished deliverables, not on the sticker.

## Where does your data go?

This is the question most comparison pages skip, and for client-data businesses it can decide the evaluation. If your work touches client contracts, financials, or health data, ask every vendor on your shortlist two things: which models run the work, and whether those providers train on your data.

On Kohai the answer is stated plainly: "Open-weight models only — DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM — from certified no-training providers." Frontier-model platforms like Lindy give you model choice per task, which is a real strength — and it's worth asking each of them the same training-data question, because the answer lives with the model provider, not the platform. A no-training certification at the model layer is the only version of that answer you can put in front of a client.

## How do you decide in one week?

Run one real, recurring task through your top two — a weekly report, an invoice chase, a lead follow-up — and score both on three numbers: time to first finished deliverable, minutes you spent setting up, and minutes you spent reviewing. The shape that matches your appetite for building will show up in the setup number; the vendor that matches your standard will show up in the deliverable.

If the setup number needs to be near zero, you've already chosen your shape — the remaining decision is which staffed team you trust with your tools and your data.
