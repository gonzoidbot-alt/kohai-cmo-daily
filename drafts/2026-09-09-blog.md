# Stop building AI agents. Hire a team instead.

Kohai is available today. Here is the idea it's built on.

If you run a small business, you've probably felt the promise and the burden of AI at the same time. The promise: software that finally does the work, not just the talking. The burden: someone has to build, prompt, wire up, and babysit every piece of it. That someone is you.

There's a whole category of tools built on this model — agent builders, workflow platforms, automation canvases. They're good at what they do, and some of them are made by sharp teams. Lindy, Relevance AI, n8n, and others have made real progress here. But they share one assumption: that you want to be the architect. You drag the nodes, you write the prompts, you debug the handoffs.

Most business owners don't want to be architects. They want the work done.

## The difference between a tool and a colleague

Think about how you'd hand work to a capable new hire. You wouldn't write them a step-by-step script. You'd brief them: "Find 50 e-commerce leads and draft the outreach." Then they'd figure out how, ask questions, and come back with something you can actually use.

That's the interaction model Kohai is built on. You describe the outcome — one message, plain language. A team assembles itself around the task: your Chief of Staff, Mochi, splits the goal and hands each piece to the right specialist, with the right tools already connected. Finished work lands in your Library — docs, spreadsheets, decks, PDFs, even deployed sites built in App Studio.

You never manage prompts. You never build an agent. You brief a colleague.

## Why "build" loses for most small teams

The build-your-own approach made sense when AI tools were primitive — the only way to get custom behavior was to assemble it yourself. But for an owner-operator, that model has three quiet costs.

**Your time.** Every agent you build is a project you manage forever. When a tool changes or a step breaks, you're the one who fixes it. The week is already full before real work starts.

**Your attention.** A builder gives you capabilities; a colleague gives you outcomes. The difference is whether you have to supervise the middle. With Kohai, you review the finished work in your Library and approve sensitive actions at an approval gate — you stay in control without watching everything.

**Your leverage.** One agent does one job. A real hire spans jobs: the Monday growth report, the invoice chase, support triage, outreach follow-ups. Kohai is built around named roles — a Sales & Growth Lead, an Operations Lead, a Marketing & Comms Lead — not single-purpose bots. In an example week on Kohai, that looks like 24 deliverables shipped and 31 hours reclaimed across sales outreach, content, ops reporting, and support.

None of this makes agent builders wrong. If you enjoy building workflows, they're great. But if you enjoy prompt engineering, Kohai removes the thing you enjoy — and that's the point.

## Private by default

One more thing that's easy to overlook: whose hands your data ends up in.

Kohai runs exclusively on open-weight models — DeepSeek, Kimi, and GLM — with inference providers certified not to train on your data, plus per-tenant isolation and encryption. That's not a bolt-on privacy page; it's the architecture. For anyone handling client information — finance, legal, healthcare-adjacent work — "our data trains your model" is a dealbreaker, and it shouldn't be.

Open-weight models also keep the pricing honest, because you're not paying frontier-model premiums for work that doesn't need them. You're paying credits for finished deliverables.

By the time you're done with your first meeting, a chunk of your week has already been done.

That's the whole loop: brief, team assembles, deliverables arrive, you approve what matters.
