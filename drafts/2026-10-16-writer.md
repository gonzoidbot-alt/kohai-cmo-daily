# What are the hidden costs of AI tools?

The subscription is rarely the whole bill. The real cost of an AI tool is the sticker price plus four lines that never appear on the pricing page: setup, integration, supervision, and surprise usage.

Those four lines are why a cheap tool ends up expensive and a dear tool ends up cheap. This post walks through each one, gives you the question that surfaces it before you buy, and ends with a five-minute audit you can run on any AI product — including ours.

## Where do the hidden costs actually sit?

Four places, and none of them show up in a price comparison. Here is the same invoice, drawn honestly:

| Hidden cost line | What it looks like in practice | The question to ask before you buy |
|---|---|---|
| Setup | Days of configuration before the first useful output | "How long until the first finished deliverable?" |
| Integration | Connecting email, calendar, CRM by hand — or running the work in the vendor's sandbox instead of your tools | "Does it work in my tools on day one, or is each connection a project?" |
| Supervision | Writing instructions, fixing outputs, re-explaining the same task every week | "Who writes the instructions — me, every time, or once?" |
| Surprise usage | A bill that scales with usage you couldn't predict | "Can I see what a task costs before I assign it?" |

A tool can win the price comparison and lose this table. The audit at the end scores all four rows in minutes.

## Why is integration the line buyers underestimate?

Because it is sold as a feature and delivered as a project. The demo runs inside the vendor's own environment; the purchase order is signed; then Gmail, Slack, Notion, and the CRM each turn out to need their own connection, their own permissions, and their own testing. For a five-person company, that is a week of someone's time before any real work gets done.

Kohai's answer is on the record: "~1,000 integrations connected day one, with per-agent tool scoping." Connected day one means the first brief runs in your actual tools, not a sandbox; per-agent scoping means each teammate touches only what you allow — which is also the governance answer to the next line.

## What does supervising AI really cost?

Often more than the subscription. A point-solution AI tool sells you capability, not a colleague: you write the instructions, review the output, and brief it again next week because it remembered nothing. Those are hours of your week spent managing software — the opposite of the hours you were trying to reclaim.

The fix is a management model, not a better instruction. On Kohai, you brief a named teammate once — Mochi, your Chief of Staff, or an Operations Lead on a schedule — and the recurring instructions persist; anything sensitive waits at an approval gate instead of either running unsupervised or demanding your attention on every step. Supervision drops from constant to a few reviewed minutes a week.

## How do you avoid the surprise-usage line?

Ask what a task costs before you assign it. Usage-priced tools hide the number until the invoice arrives; the honest version quotes the work up front — on Kohai, credits are the unit, a blog post quotes in the dozens, a deck in the low hundreds, and you see the quote before the team starts.

Also ask what happens when the allowance runs out mid-month. A tool that pauses and tells you is being straight with you; one that quietly meters overage is not. Whichever vendor you choose, get both answers during the trial — not after the first invoice.

## How do you audit an AI tool in five minutes?

Run the table against the real product, before the trial converts you:

1. **Time to first deliverable.** From signup to a finished result in your Library — minutes, not days. If onboarding is a project, that is the setup line arriving early.
2. **Count the connections.** List the three tools your work actually lives in. If each needs a build, the integration line is real.
3. **Ask who writes the instructions.** Once per role, or once per task, forever? The second answer is a salary you didn't budget.
4. **Ask for a task quote.** One blog post, one report, one pipeline sweep — priced before you assign them, or not?
5. **Ask the overage question.** Pause or meter? Get it in writing.

Five honest answers beat any comparison matrix. If a tool clears all five, the 7-day free trial is the confirmation: brief one real task on day one and check that the deliverable lands — finished, in your tools, at the quoted price.
