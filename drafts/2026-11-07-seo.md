## Fix 1 — Severity: HIGH. No page-level structured data anywhere: add FAQPage schema to /pricing (content already on-page)

Every page serves only `Organization` + `ImageObject` + `WebSite` JSON-LD (verified on /, /pricing, /blog/*). The pricing page ships a full 9-question FAQ in the DOM, and every `/vs/*` page has an "answered" FAQ section — but **zero FAQPage, Product/Offer, or Article markup exists sitewide**. Consequences: no FAQ rich-result eligibility on the highest-intent page (/pricing), and competitors' pricing/comparison content (Lindy publishes pricing-takedown posts; Relevance AI has docs-first structured content) is more extractable by both Google and AI engines. This is the highest-leverage fix because the content already exists — it's purely a markup job.

Copy-paste — add to `/pricing` (head, alongside existing JSON-LD array; Q&A text taken verbatim from the live page):

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "FAQPage",
  "mainEntity": [
    {
      "@type": "Question",
      "name": "What is a credit in Kōhai?",
      "acceptedAnswer": { "@type": "Answer", "text": "A credit is your unit of usage, the way Kōhai abstracts away tokens, model time and tool calls. Most small tasks (a quick research query, a short email, a paragraph rewrite) use 5 to 10 credits. Medium tasks (a chart, a landing page, a blog post) use 20 to 50. Large jobs (a deck, market research, building something) use 100 to 300." }
    },
    {
      "@type": "Question",
      "name": "How much does Kōhai cost per month?",
      "acceptedAnswer": { "@type": "Answer", "text": "Kōhai is $20/month with 8,000 credits, Kōhai Pro is $100/month with 40,000 credits and Kōhai Max is $200/month with 80,000 credits. Kōhai Free is $0 for a limited time. Every plan includes unlimited team seats, the whole AI team, every role template and every integration." }
    },
    {
      "@type": "Question",
      "name": "Do Kōhai credits roll over?",
      "acceptedAnswer": { "@type": "Answer", "text": "Monthly credits don't roll over; each cycle starts with a clean allowance. Add-on credits, the ones you buy on top, never expire." }
    }
  ]
}
```

Then repeat the pattern: FAQPage on each `/vs/*` page (from its "answered" section) and `Article` + `BlogPosting` schema on the 21 blog posts. If Astro templates drive these layouts, this is one component edit per layout, not 30 page edits.

## Fix 2 — Severity: MEDIUM-HIGH. /vs/* page metadata misses the "alternative" query intent it was built to capture

The `/vs` hub is the right asset (16 competitor pages, matching the strategy's money keywords "Lindy alternative", "Artisan alternative"), but the metadata doesn't speak the query language. Verified on `/vs/lindy`: title is `Kōhai vs. Lindy: which should you choose?` and the meta description is `One AI employee in Slack, priced per person, vs. a whole team on one plan.` — neither contains "Lindy alternative", the phrase searchers actually type and AI engines hear. The FAQ on-page can't compensate if the title/description never match the query.

Copy-paste — `/vs/lindy` (facts consistent with the page's own copy):

```html
<title>Kōhai vs. Lindy (2026): a no-workflow-building Lindy alternative | Kōhai</title>
<meta name="description" content="Looking for a Lindy alternative? Kōhai gives you a whole AI team on one plan with unlimited seats, instead of one AI employee per seat. Compare pricing, privacy and how the work gets done.">
```

And `/vs/artisan`:

```html
<title>Kōhai vs. Artisan (2026): a self-serve Artisan alternative | Kōhai</title>
<meta name="description" content="Looking for an Artisan AI alternative? Kōhai starts free with no card and no sales call — a full team of AI teammates from day one. Compare pricing and approach side by side.">
```

Apply the same `<competitor> alternative` pattern to all 16 `/vs/*` titles + descriptions. Keep the generous, factual comparison tone per the design guide — this is keyword placement, not trash talk.

## Keyword-gap observation — "AI employees for small business" is owned by no page title

Per 04-content-strategy.md, the #1 money keyword is **"AI employees for small business"**. Today no page title contains it. The closest asset is `/blog/what-is-an-ai-employee` — the **slug** targets "AI employee" but the **title** targets "AI teammate": `What is an AI teammate? | Kōhai Blog`. That's a self-inflicted split: the URL signals one keyword, the title another, and neither matches the exact money phrase ("AI employees for small business" / "hire AI employees"). `/blog/best-ai-agents-for-small-business` titles to "AI teams for small business" and `/blog/ai-for-small-business` to "AI for small business" — adjacent, not exact.

Suggested retitle (keeps brand voice, adds the exact query):

```html
<title>What is an AI employee? (And why small businesses hire teams of them) | Kōhai Blog</title>
```

Longer-term: the brand deliberately says "teammates/colleagues" on-page — fine — but at least one crawlable page should carry the exact "AI employees for small business" phrase in `<title>` + H1 for the engines; the blog post above is the natural home, or ship the planned `/ai-colleagues` anchor page retitled for the "employee" query family.
