# The weekly pipeline sweep: how to keep your CRM honest without living in it

A weekly pipeline sweep is the cheapest fix for a CRM that's quietly drifting: every open deal gets checked for silence, missing next steps, and close dates that already passed. You can run it yourself in an hour each week — or brief an AI colleague to run it every Wednesday and land the report in your Library before you're back from lunch.

## What does a pipeline sweep actually check?

A sweep is not selling. It's a hygiene pass over every open deal, and it looks for the same five things every time:

1. **Silence** — no touch in or out for 14 days or more
2. **Missing next step** — no call, demo, or follow-up on the calendar
3. **Stale close date** — the date is in the past, or says "someday"
4. **Ghosted thread** — you sent the proposal, they never replied, nothing is queued
5. **Stuck stage** — the deal has sat in one stage longer than your usual cycle

None of these mean the deal is dead. They mean it's unattended — and unattended is how deals actually die.

## Why does a pipeline rot in the first place?

Deals rarely fall apart loudly. They fade while you're delivering the work, answering support, and doing the ten other jobs a small business runs on. The CRM becomes a place where you record what already happened instead of a working list of what happens next.

For a solo operator, sales is the function most likely to be run from memory — and memory is exactly what a pipeline sweep replaces. The CRM already knows which deals went quiet. A sweep is just the discipline of asking it every week, on a schedule, before the quiet becomes a lost quarter.

## What's the difference between doing it yourself and briefing it?

| | Manual sweep (you, Friday afternoon) | Briefed sweep (AI colleague, weekly) |
|---|---|---|
| When it happens | When the week leaves you time | Wednesday, 2:00 pm, every week |
| What gets checked | Whatever you remember to look at | All five checks, on every open deal |
| What you see | Your own notes, reconstructed | A flagged-deal report in your Library |
| Follow-ups | Written by you, one by one | Drafted for you, held for approval |
| What slips | The quiet deals | Nothing — silence is itself a flag |

The manual version works. That's the trap — it works just well enough that the quiet deals stay quiet for another week. And on the weeks it doesn't happen at all, nothing flags the gap, because the person who would do the sweep is the person the week swallowed.

## What does the brief look like?

One message, the way you'd brief a capable hire:

> "You're my Sales & Growth Lead. Every Wednesday at 2pm, go through every open deal in the CRM. Flag anything with no touch in 14 days, no next step scheduled, or a close date that's already passed. For each flagged deal, draft a short, warm nudge — these are relationships, not reminders — and hold every draft for my approval before anything sends. Leave the report in my Library by 3pm."

No workflow to diagram, no automation rules to maintain. One message is the whole job description, and corrections are sentences: "skip anything under $1,000," "flag at 10 days during launch month," "CC me on the two biggest accounts."

## Who sees the data in your CRM?

A pipeline sweep reads the most personal data in your business: customer names, deal sizes, the things prospects said in private threads. That deserves a straight answer.

Kohai's answer is on the record: "Open-weight models only — DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM — from certified no-training providers." Your pipeline isn't training anyone's model, and per-tenant isolation keeps it separated from every other customer's.

Scoping goes both ways, too: "~1,000 integrations connected day one, with per-agent tool scoping." Your Sales & Growth Lead reaches the CRM and the inbox — and doesn't reach your accounting or your codebase. You decide which apps each colleague touches, which means the sweep reads exactly the deal data it needs and nothing else.

## What does a swept week produce?

By Wednesday afternoon you have a short list: the deals that went quiet, the drafts that answer them, and nothing else pretending to be urgent. You read, you approve, you move on — the whole pass costs minutes instead of an afternoon.

Over a month, that's the difference between a pipeline you audit and a pipeline you actually work. The sweep doesn't close deals for you. It just makes sure none of them slip away unattended.

## Run one sweep and compare

Take the 7-day free trial, brief the sweep as a recurring Wednesday task, and let one report land in your Library. If the flagged list matches what you would have caught on your own — minus the Friday afternoon it would have cost — keep it on the schedule.

Your CRM is only as honest as its last review. A colleague who reviews it weekly is the cheapest honesty you'll ever buy.
