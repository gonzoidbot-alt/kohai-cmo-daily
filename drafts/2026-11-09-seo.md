## Priority Fix 1 (HIGH) — Missing structured data (schema.org)

**Severity: High** — Blocks rich snippets in search results and limits AI-engine citation surface area.

The site has Organization + Website JSON-LD on every page, but is missing several schema types that competitors (Lindy, Artisan, Sintra) implement for enhanced SERP features.

### Fix: Add these schema types site-wide

**A. BreadcrumbList** on every inner page (/pricing, /blog/*, /guides/*, /solutions/*, /vs/*)

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "BreadcrumbList",
  "itemListElement": [
    { "@type": "ListItem", "position": 1, "name": "Kōhai", "item": "https://kohai.co/" },
    { "@type": "ListItem", "position": 2, "name": "Pricing", "item": "https://kohai.co/pricing" }
  ]
}
```

**B. FAQPage** on /pricing (the "Pricing questions, answered" section is a natural FAQ schema candidate):

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "FAQPage",
  "mainEntity": [

      "@type": "Question",

Large jobs (a deck, market research, building something) use 100 to 300."

**D. BlogPosting / Article** on each blog post page — includes author (Alex Morgan? — verify from the post), datePublished, dateModified, and image (the blog card uses `/images/kamon/*.svg` as crest images — use the hero or a featured image instead). This is the single highest-impact schema add because it unlocks article-rich results, author info in knowledge panels, and speakable markup for AI assistants.

**Current blog posts lack any article-specific structured data.** Implementation snippet:

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "BlogPosting",
  "headline": "Teammates, not coworkers",
  "description": "The industry keeps picking nouns for AI. We chose teammate, and the choice shapes the whole product.",
  "datePublished": "2026-08-07",

    "@type": "Person",
    "name": "Kōhai Team"
  },
  "publisher": {
    "@type": "Organization",
    "name": "Kōhai",
    "logo": { "@type": "ImageObject", "url": "https://kohai.co/images/brand/logo.svg" }

```

**Implementation approach:** Since the site is Astro SSG, schema should be injected either via component (per-page JSON-LD), or better, as a dynamic `<script type="application/ld+json">` block in the blog layout template that reads from frontmatter. The current Organization + Website schema is already inlined — extend that pattern.

## Priority Fix 3 (MEDIUM) — Credit-to-hours translation gap on pricing page

**Severity: Medium** — The pricing FAQ explains credits well for existing users, but new visitors (ICP-1 solopreneurs) compare "8,000 credits" against Sintra's "250 credits" or Marblism's "50 hours" and have no anchor. This creates an unnecessary cognitive load at the decision point.

The blog post "The predictable AI bill" (2026-07-02) and "What an AI job actually costs" (2026-06-11) do this translation well, but the pricing page itself doesn't.

**Fix:** Add a "What does 8,000 credits get me?" comparison line under each plan card. Exact language suggestion:

**Under Kōhai card ($20/8,000 credits):**
> "8,000 credits ≈ 100–400 small tasks, 160–400 medium tasks, or 25–80 large jobs per month"

**Under Kōhai Pro card ($100/40,000 credits):**
> "40,000 credits ≈ 500–2,000 small tasks, 800–2,000 medium tasks, or 130–400 large jobs per month"

**Under Kōhai Max card ($200/80,000 credits):**
> "80,000 credits ≈ 1,000–4,000 small tasks, 1,600–4,000 medium tasks, or 260–800 large jobs per month"

*(Ranges based on verified costs: small 5–10 credits, medium 20–50, large 100–300 from 02-positioning-icp.md § Key claims.)*

Alternatively, an "hour equivalent" line:
> "≈ 31 hours reclaimed per week (Kōhai's example week) — more output than a part-time hire"

This directly maps to Claim #1 (24 deliverables / 31 hours) and gives the buyer an immediate mental model.

## Keyword Gap Observation

### Gap 1: "AI employees for [role]" long-tail queries

**Observation:** The live site has excellent solution pages (/solutions/sales, /solutions/marketing, /solutions/customer-support, etc.) and a deep guides library, but these pages are discoverable mostly via footer navigation and llms.txt, not surfaced strongly from the homepage or blog. The 16 comparison pages (/vs/) are comprehensive but live at a URL depth that search engines may not prioritize over competitors' simpler, more directly-named pages.

**What competitors are doing:** Lindy runs SEO takedown content ranking for "Sintra AI Pricing" (mentioned in 01-competitor-analysis.md). Marblism's "act first" framing blog + press release strategy. Sintra's affiliate army and blog/case-study push.

**Recommendation:** Build internal links from the most-trafficked blog posts to the most relevant solution or comparison pages. For example:
- The "best AI tools for business" blog post should link to /solutions/smb, /solutions/solopreneurs, and /solutions/sales (already partially done — /blog/best-ai-tools-for-business mentions them inline).
- The comparison table blog post (/blog/best-ai-agents-for-small-business) should link to all 16 /vs/* pages.
- New blog posts should always link to at least one solution or comparison page in the body.

This is a low-effort, high-impact content silo fix.

### Gap 2: "Lindy alternative" — pricing shift advantage

**Observation:** The 01-competitor-analysis.md (Sep 8) records Lindy at $29.99–$199.99/user/3k–35k credits. The live /vs/lindy page (updated Oct 22) shows Lindy's current pricing as "$49.99 per user a month for 5,000 credits" — significantly more expensive per credit than when the strategy doc was written.

**This is a direct opportunity.** The /vs/lindy page already makes the "team vs one assistant" argument well, but it can be sharpened with the latest pricing delta:
- Kohai Pro: $100/mo for the whole team, 40,000 credits, unlimited seats
- Lindy: ~$50/user/mo for 5,000 credits, per-user billing

Updated copy suggestion for /vs/lindy top section:
> "At ~$50/user for 5k credits, a 3-person team on Lindy costs as much as Kōhai Pro — but gets one assistant per person instead of a staffed team with 8× the monthly capacity."

### Gap 3: "AI workplace" / "AI workforce" search positioning

**Observation:** Relevance AI owns "AI Workforce" in enterprise search (see 01-competitor-analysis.md). Kohai's positioning uses "colleagues", "teammates", and "workforce" less prominently on the homepage than it should.

**Fix:** Strengthen the "AI workforce" signal in the homepage hero and meta description. The current H1 is "Onboard AI teammates. Get work finished." — good, but the subheading adds "collaborative AI workspace" which is less searchable. Consider adding a more explicit workforce phrase in the hero subtitle or meta description.

**Current meta description:** "Kōhai puts a collaborative AI workspace inside every business, fully staffed with AI teammates from day one."
**Suggested update:** "Kōhai gives you a private AI workforce — a fully staffed team of AI teammates that work in your tools and hand you finished results. Start free."

### Gap 4: Missing pricing keyword targets

**Observation:** The pricing page has excellent FAQ content but is invisible to anyone searching for:
- "Kōhai pricing" (obviously — but ensure it's indexable and canonical)
- "AI employee cost per month"
- "how much does an AI workforce cost"
- "cheapest AI employee platform"

These queries are addressed in individual blog posts but not on the pricing page itself. The FAQ section is the right place. Consider adding a "How does Kōhai compare on price?" FAQ that links to the comparison pages.

### Gap 5: Blog internal linking audit

**Observation:** The blog has 15+ posts in a flat taxonomy. No topic clusters. No pillar page strategy. Each post links to the product but rarely to each other. This limits topical authority building.

**Recommended cluster structure (low effort, high SEO return):**

| Pillar Topic | Cluster Posts |
|---|---|
| AI pricing / cost | predictable-ai-bill, what-an-ai-job-actually-costs, what-is-an-open-weight-model, never-download-the-model |
| AI teammates vs alternatives | best-ai-agents-for-small-business, best-ai-tools-for-business, ai-employee-vs-virtual-assistant-vs-freelancer, introducing-kohai, what-is-an-ai-employee |
| Using AI in business | ai-for-small-business, how-ai-employees-help-every-company-size, work-is-multiplayer, stop-building-ai-agents, colleagues-not-coworkers |
| Product / company | kohai-launch, kohai-is-live-early-access-is-open, early-access-update-june-2026, replacement-trap, the-ai-usage-memo-you-shouldnt-have-to-send |

Each pillar page should link to its cluster articles; each cluster article should link back to the pillar.
