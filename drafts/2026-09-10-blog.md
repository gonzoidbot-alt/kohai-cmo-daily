# Why we built Kohai on open-weight models

When you hire someone, you make two decisions before their first day: what they can access, and what they cost. We think hiring AI colleagues should work the same way. That's why Kohai runs exclusively on open-weight models — DeepSeek, Kimi, and GLM — and why our pricing is credits, not tokens.

This post explains the three reasons: privacy, predictable cost, and independence. It also covers what we give up, because the trade-offs are real.

## First, what "open-weight" means

A model has weights — the trained parameters that make it work. Open-weight models publish those weights, so anyone can run them on their own infrastructure. Frontier models from the big labs are closed: you send your data to their API and trust their word on what happens next.

Running open-weight models doesn't mean running cheap or outdated ones. It means the model isn't chained to one company's servers, terms, or pricing.

## Reason 1: your work stays yours

Kohai's inference providers are certified not to train on your data. Your briefs, your client documents, your pipeline numbers — they don't become someone else's training set.

On top of that, every workspace is isolated per tenant and encrypted. Your Marketing & Comms Lead's drafts and your Operations Lead's reports live in your tenant, not a shared pool.

If you handle client data — finance, legal, healthcare-adjacent work — this is usually the first question you ask a vendor. "Open-weight only, no-training providers, per-tenant isolation" is a sentence you can forward to the person who asks it.

## Reason 2: you can see what work costs before you assign it

Frontier API pricing is per token. Per-token pricing means your bill depends on how verbose a model was on a Tuesday. That's not a budget; that's a guess.

Kohai prices work in credits:

- $29.99/mo — 12,000 credits, 1 team seat
- $99.99/mo (Pro) — 40,000 credits, 3 team seats
- $199.99/mo (Max) — 80,000 credits, unlimited seats

A blog post runs 20–50 credits. A deck runs 100–300. That means on the base plan, 12,000 credits covers a lot of weekly reports, outreach drafts, and meeting summaries — and you can size the work before you brief it, not after the invoice lands.

The example week on our site: 24 deliverables shipped, 31 hours reclaimed. You can hold that against the credit cost and decide for yourself whether the math works for your week. We'd rather you do that arithmetic than take our word for it.

## Reason 3: independence from one lab's roadmap

When your whole product runs on one provider's closed model, their decisions become your decisions. Price changes, rate limits, deprecations, terms updates — you find out when it breaks your workflow.

Open-weight models spread that risk. DeepSeek, Kimi, and GLM are maintained by different organizations, and open weights mean any qualified provider can serve them. If one lab changes course, we're not stuck. Neither are you.

There's a second benefit: providers compete for the work. That competition is part of how a $29.99 plan funds a full team of colleagues instead of a chat window.

## What we trade off

Honesty section, because you'd ask anyway.

Frontier labs publish the biggest benchmark numbers, and "open-weight only" reads as a limitation if you assume biggest always means best for your task. For the work Kohai's colleagues do — research, drafting, outreach, reporting, triage, planning — open-weight models are strong, and they improve with every release. But if your workflow needs one specific frontier capability, we're not the right fit today, and we'd rather say that than oversell.

We also don't let you pick the underlying model. You brief colleagues, not models — you tell your Strategy Lead what answer you need, and the routing is our job. If you enjoy hand-picking models per task, you'd be happier with a builder tool. That's a real segment of the market; it's just not who we're for.

## How this connects to everything else

Open-weight is one of three commitments we make by default. The other two:

1. **Approval gates.** Sensitive actions — the invoice chase, the outbound email, the follow-up — wait at an approval gate until you say go. Autonomy is a dial you control, not a mode you hope behaves.
2. **Scoped access.** Around 1,000 integrations connect on day one, and each colleague only touches the tools you allow. Per-agent scoping, set at onboarding.

Private by default, predictable by design. The three ideas reinforce each other: open weights make private inference practical, and both make credit pricing stable enough to publish.

## Try it and check the receipts

Kohai has a 7-day free trial, and the first deliverable lands in your Library the same day. Brief a colleague the way you'd brief a new hire, watch what it costs in credits, and see whether the numbers hold up in your week.

We built on open-weight models because it lets you answer the two questions every good hire answers: what can this person see, and what do they cost. If those answers matter to you, that's the product.
