# The Best AI Employees for Small Business: The Five Shapes of the Category, Compared

The best AI employee platform for a small business is the one whose shape matches how you want to work — and the category has settled into five distinct shapes you can tell apart in an afternoon. This post maps all five, describes each fairly on its own terms, and gives you one table to compare them before you commit a budget line.

"AI employee" has become an umbrella over some very different products. Some are tools you build. Some are hires you brief. Some are characters you chat with. The label alone tells you almost nothing, which is why comparison posts that stop at a pricing table mislead more than they help. Start with the shape, then check the price.

## Shape 1: Builder platforms — powerful, and yours to assemble

Builder platforms — Lindy and Relevance AI are the most visible examples — give you a workbench for constructing AI workflows yourself. Lindy's pricing page is candid about the model: every active user in your workspace is billed, starting at $29.99 a month for 3,000 credits each, scaling through credit tiers up to enterprise volumes. Relevance AI has moved its higher tiers to a sales-led enterprise motion. Both are capable products with real depth — Lindy advertises 3,000+ integrations and per-task model selection, and Relevance AI documents its platform extensively.

The trade isn't capability; it's who does the building. On a builder platform, the product is what you assemble, which means the work of designing, testing, and maintaining the automations is yours. For technical teams, that's the point. For a founder who is already the whole ops department, assembly time is the tax.

## Shape 2: Vertical GTM hires — deep in one function

Artisan takes a different bet: hire deep in one function rather than broadly across all of them. Its AI sales hires come with white-glove onboarding, CRM setup, and forward-deployed strategists — a genuinely higher-touch motion than anything self-serve in this category. Pricing is credit-based with tiers from startup through enterprise, presented through a sales conversation rather than a public checkout.

If outbound sales is the one function eating your week, a vertical hire is a reasonable shape. The trade is coverage: one function gets a specialist, and the other four still need a solution.

## Shape 3: Persona helpers — the entry price of the category

Sintra sells a team of a dozen named helpers — Penn for copywriting, Cassie for support, Scouty for recruiting — at the category's friendliest entry price: $48.50/month on the monthly plan, dropping to $15.60/month on the annual commitment, with 250 monthly credits and 15+ integrations. It's an honest product at an honest price for getting a feel for delegated AI work.

The trade is depth. A helper you chat with produces chat-scale output; a colleague who holds a brief produces deliverables. If you're testing whether delegated AI work fits your week at all, Sintra is a low-cost place to find out.

## Shape 4: Proactive flat-rate teams — hours, not credits

Marblism prices differently from everyone above: a flat subscription from $24/month billed yearly for a 50-hours-per-month base, with unlimited team members and named employees who are designed to act first — drafting, posting, answering — without waiting to be asked. Its benchmark page is refreshingly specific about approval behavior, and the hours-based model makes the bill easy to predict.

The trade is the control model. When the work starts without a brief, your oversight has to live in review habits rather than at the moment of action. Some operators love that; for others it's the reason they're shopping.

## Shape 5: Task-first colleagues — you brief, they deliver, you approve

The fifth shape inverts shape 4. A task-first platform like Kōhai starts work only when you brief it — one message describing the outcome, the way you'd hand work to a new hire — and sensitive actions wait at an approval gate until you say go. You keep the trigger; the colleague keeps the labor.

Kōhai runs exclusively on open-weight models — DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM — from inference providers certified not to train on your data, which is why its plans can be flat-rate: Kōhai Free is $0 for a limited time (first 1,000 users), then $20/month for 8,000 credits, $100/month Pro for 40,000, and $200/month Max for 80,000, with unlimited seats on every plan. Work lands as finished deliverables in your Library — documents, decks, spreadsheets, even deployed software — and failed tasks consume no credits.

**"~1,000 integrations connected day one, with per-agent tool scoping."** The scoping half of that matters more than the breadth: each colleague touches only the tools you've scoped to it, so "connected to everything" never means "able to act on everything."

## So which shape is best for a small business?

The honest answer runs through two questions, not one.

First: who starts the work? If you want to design the workflows, a builder platform rewards the effort with the most control over mechanics. If you want to hand off outcomes, the hired-team shapes (2, 4, 5) are the fit, and the difference between them is who holds the trigger — a proactive team starts on its own; task-first colleagues wait for your brief.

Second: what does the bill do when you grow? Per-seat models multiply with every function you add. Flat-rate models don't — adding a Marketing Lead and a Support Lead to a flat-credit plan changes the team, not the invoice. For a five-person company covering five functions, that single row of the table often decides the comparison.

A founder who wants outcomes without assembling them, coverage across every function rather than depth in one, and a bill that stays flat as the team grows is describing shape 5. If that's you, Kōhai's free plan costs nothing to test — and your first deliverable is in your Library the same day you brief it.
