# Relevance AI Alternative: When You Want the Work, Not the Workflow

If you're searching for a Relevance AI alternative, the honest first question isn't "which platform is better." It's whether building was ever the job you wanted. Relevance AI is a genuinely strong builder platform: you compose agents, connect them to your data and tools, and run them as an AI workforce. Kohai is a different shape entirely — you brief a staffed team in one message, the team does the work, and finished deliverables land in your Library. This post compares the two on the axis that decides the purchase for most small teams: who assembles the system, who maintains it, and what actually exists at the end of your first week.

## What Relevance AI Does Well

Credit where it's earned, because a fair comparison starts there. Relevance AI's economics are unusually transparent for this category: it bills on two meters — Actions (one per tool run) and Vendor Credits (model and tool costs passed through near wholesale) — and it lets you bring your own API keys. If you want to see exactly what the machine layer costs, that design shows you. The company is also further along on enterprise readiness than most of this category: SOC 2 Type II compliance, and on its current pricing page, SSO, RBAC, audit logs, agent evaluations, and a dedicated account manager at the Enterprise tier. And the builder itself is deep: custom actions, unlimited agents and tools, calling and meeting agents, A/B testing.

If your team has an ops or RevOps person who enjoys composing systems, Relevance AI is a defensible choice. The rest of this post is about the buyer who doesn't.

## Who Assembles the System

The structural difference is a single question: in the product's default flow, who is the architect?

With a builder platform, you are. You choose the model per agent, wire the triggers, write the instructions, test the edge cases, and debug the chain when a step breaks at 11 p.m. The platform provides excellent tooling; the design labor, the setup time, and the ongoing maintenance are yours. That's not a flaw — it's the product. It's what "builder" means.

With Kohai, the team arrives assembled. Six named leads — Strategy, Sales & Growth, Marketing & Comms, Customer Support, Operations, plus dozens of role templates — are already staffed and connected to roughly 1,000 integrations on day one. Your labor is the brief: one message that describes the outcome, the way you'd brief a capable hire. "Find 50 e-commerce leads and draft the outreach" is a complete job description. You never open a canvas, because there isn't one.

## What You Maintain After Launch

| | Builder platform (Relevance AI) | Brief-first team (Kohai) |
|---|---|---|
| Setup labor | You compose agents and logic | One-message brief |
| When something breaks | You debug the workflow | The platform's team fixes the run; a failed task consumes 0 credits |
| Skill required | Workflow design | Knowing what you want |
| Who owns the design | You do, permanently | Kohai does |
| Best fit | Teams with builder capacity | Operators who want output |

The row that decides most purchases is the second one. Maintenance is the silent subscription fee on any assembled system. Agents drift, integrations change their APIs, prompts rot as models update. On a builder platform, every one of those is your ticket. On a brief-first platform, the run happens inside the product and a failed task costs you nothing.

## What "Done" Looks Like

Here's the test most comparisons skip: at the end of week one, what exists?

On a builder platform, the honest answer is often "a promising system in staging." That's real progress, and for the right buyer it's the point. But it isn't a deliverable yet.

On Kohai, the unit of value is the finished artifact — a doc, a deck, a spreadsheet, a sequence, a PDF. Work lands in your Library where you review it, and anything sensitive waits at an approval gate until you say go. You judge your AI hire the way you'd judge a new employee: by the work, not by the wiring.

And when the outcome you want *is* software — an internal tool, a landing page, a client portal — the gap widens. Most platforms stop at a mockup or a prototype. *"App Studio ships deployed software, not mockups."* Ask any platform you're evaluating what happens after you approve the build: a file you host yourself, or a live URL. That single question separates the category faster than any feature matrix.

## Pricing, Side by Side

| | Relevance AI (as of this writing) | Kohai |

| Entry tier | Pro — $19/mo annual ($29 monthly), 2,500 Actions + $20 Vendor Credits/mo, 2 build users | Kōhai — $20/mo, 8,000 credits |
| Mid tier | Team — $234/mo annual ($349 monthly), 7,000 Actions + $70 Vendor Credits/mo | Kōhai Pro — $100/mo, 40,000 credits |
| Free plan | Retired for new and existing accounts | Kōhai Free — $0, no card, credits refresh monthly (limited time, first 1,000 users) |
| Seats | Build users tiered; end users on Team+ | Unlimited seats on every plan |
| Billing model | Two meters (Actions + Vendor Credits) | One meter (credits), costs published per task type |

Two notes on fairness. First, Relevance AI's pass-through credit model is genuinely cheaper at the margin for high-volume, well-tuned workloads — that's the trade it's offering: your design labor for their transparency. Second, Relevance AI's public pricing page currently leads with its Enterprise tier, so confirm current self-serve terms directly before you compare numbers. Kohai's published costs are small tasks at 5–10 credits, medium work like a blog post or landing page at 20–50, and large deliverables like decks and deep research at 100–300 — you can price a month before you spend it.

## Who Should Pick Which

Pick Relevance AI if you want to compose the system yourself, need enterprise governance today, or run high-volume automations where pass-through credit economics win. It's a good product for that buyer, and this is not a close call in its favor territory.

Pick Kohai if the thought of maintaining a workflow canvas makes you tired, if your week is already full before the maintenance starts, or if what you actually want is finished work in a Library — not a system to babysit.

The simplest version: Relevance AI sells you the factory. Kohai ships you the goods. If you've bounced off a builder once already, you already know which one you are.

Start on Kōhai Free — $0, no card — and brief your first teammate today. The first deliverable lands in your Library before dinner.
