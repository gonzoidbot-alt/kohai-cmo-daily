# Which AI model should your business use?

If you're hiring a team of AI colleagues, the honest answer is none — and having that decision leave your desk is the point. If you're buying raw model time to run workflows yourself, the choice matters, and this post is about where that line sits.

Lots of founders never notice they've crossed it. They start by comparing tools, and one tab later they're comparing models — benchmarks, context windows, pricing pages that expire monthly. That is model choice arriving as a second job with no title.

## What does owning model choice actually cost?

The one-time pick is the smallest part. The cost is that the pick keeps coming due.

| When it recurs | What you're doing | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| First evaluation | Comparing benchmarks and pricing pages | An afternoon of research with an expiry date |
| Every major release | Re-running the comparison | A recurring obligation, roughly monthly |
| Every new task | Rewriting prompts for each model's quirks | The per-task tax |
| Every result | Adjusting your quality bar per model | Reviewing work twice |

None of this is wrong for people who enjoy it. Model-watching is genuinely interesting, and for some businesses the model *is* the product. But if your week is already full of sales, content, ops, and support, it's unmanaged recurring work — exactly the category of labor you hired software to absorb in the first place.

## What do you actually decide when you hire instead?

Four things, and all of them stay yours: the outcome, the standard of done, the schedule, and what counts as sensitive. Everything under those — the steps, the tool routing, the model — becomes the colleague's job.

| Stays yours | Leaves your desk |
|---|---|
| The outcome — one message is the whole job description | The steps between brief and deliverable |
| The standard — what "finished" looks like | Which tools each task touches |
| The schedule — when it runs | Which model does the work |
| The approval gate — what waits for your yes | Maintaining the machinery that produces it |

This is the ordinary logic of hiring, and it's why we say **brief it like a colleague**. You don't tell a new hire which part of their brain to use or re-interview them every time a release ships. You tell them what you need, when you need it, and where the line is. The model question isn't dodged — it's one of the things a hire is supposed to carry so you don't have to.

## So what are your colleagues actually running on?

A stack chosen once and disclosed, not re-argued every release cycle. On Kohai: "Open-weight models only — DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM — from certified no-training providers."

Each half of that sentence does work. "Open-weight" means the model publishes its weights, so its behavior can be inspected rather than merely asserted — these are real, current models, not cut-down stand-ins; the roles are simply not built around any one vendor's flagship. "Certified no-training providers" means the inference providers behind your work are certified not to train on your data, and the whole thing runs under per-tenant isolation with encryption.

Skipping the choice, in other words, is not the same as being in the dark. It means the choice was made deliberately, published plainly, and can be checked — which is a better position than most "we use the best available model" pages give you, because "best available" is a sentence that changes meaning every month.

## When should you pick the model yourself?

More often than most product pages will admit. There are buyers for whom model choice is a genuine strength, and the builder platforms that offer it per task are honestly built for them.

| Pick your own model when… | Hire colleagues when… |
|---|---|
| Model choice is your craft — or your product | You want the work, not the stack |
| A workflow genuinely needs one specific frontier capability | The work is research, drafting, outreach, reporting, triage |
| You enjoy tuning prompts and pipelines | You'd rather brief an outcome and review deliverables |

If you read the left column and feel recognized, buy the left column — a builder platform will reward you. If you read the right column and exhale, that relief is the signal. It means the decision that's been shadowing your week is one you're ready to hand over.

## What your first week looks like

Write the outcome as one message — the whole job description. Attach it to a schedule so the work recurs without re-briefing. Mark whatever sends, spends, or touches customers as sensitive, so it waits at an approval gate until you say go. The first finished deliverable lands in your Library the same day.

That's the entire decision set. What model did the work is a checkable fact about the run, not a standing question on your list. You stay in control of the outcome, the standard, and the gate — and the tabs full of benchmark comparisons can close.
