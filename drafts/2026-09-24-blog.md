# What an AI chief of staff actually does in a week

An AI chief of staff handles the work that surrounds your meetings: the prep, the brief, the notes, the summary, and the follow-ups. You do the meeting. It does everything else on the agenda's edges.

That's the practical answer to a question more founders are asking as calendars, not code, become the bottleneck. Here's what the role looks like day to day — using Mochi, Kohai's Chief of Staff, as the worked example.

## The problem it solves

Look at a founder's calendar and count the work attached to each meeting. The research you meant to do. The notes you take badly while trying to actually listen. The summary you never write. The follow-ups that live in your head until they're late.

For a solo operator, this work has no owner. You can't hire a chief of staff at $5k a month, and a note-taking app only handles one slice of it. The rest still lands on you — usually at 10pm.

The ICP math is blunt: if meetings and their admin eat 5–8 hours a week, that's a workday you're spending on work about work.

## Brief, don't prompt

You don't configure Mochi. You brief it — the same way you'd brief a capable human hire on their first day.

"Sit in on my meetings, keep me prepared, and make sure nothing said in them falls through the cracks." One message. Mochi connects to your calendar and inbox, learns the rhythm of your week, and starts working. No workflow diagrams, no prompt engineering, no build session.

This is the difference between hiring and building. Agent-builder tools make you the architect; Kohai makes you the manager. If you've ever onboarded a person, you already know how to run this.

## The 5-step meeting flow

Mochi runs every meeting through the same loop. Here it is end to end:

1. **Preps the meeting.** It reads the attendees, the context, and your past threads with them, and drafts what you need to walk in knowing.
2. **Briefs you 15 minutes before.** A short brief arrives before the meeting starts — who they are, what's open, what you committed to last time.
3. **Takes live notes.** During the call, it captures what was said so you can listen instead of typing.
4. **Writes the summary.** Afterward, a clean summary lands in your Library: decisions, owners, dates.
5. **Starts the follow-ups.** It drafts the follow-up emails and next actions. Anything sensitive — a commitment made on your behalf, an email going to a client — waits at the approval gate until you say go.

Notice where the approval gate sits. Mochi does the reading, writing, and remembering. You make the sends. That's not a limitation; it's the design. Trust comes from control at the sensitive moments, not from autonomy you have to audit.

## What you actually see

The deliverables show up in your Library, not in a chat log you have to scroll. A meeting brief before the call. A summary after it. Follow-up drafts queued for your review.

This matters more than it sounds. Tools that live in a chat window make you the dispatcher — you have to ask, read, and copy things out. A colleague delivers finished work to a place where finished work belongs.

## What a week looks like

Zoom out from one meeting to a full week of this running quietly. Briefs arriving on schedule. Summaries filing themselves. Follow-ups drafted and waiting. Add in the rest of the team — reporting, outreach drafts, support triage — and the numbers compound.

Kohai's example week: 24 deliverables shipped, 31 hours reclaimed. The meeting flow is a meaningful share of that, because meeting admin is the kind of recurring, well-defined work AI colleagues handle best — and the kind founders never get to delegate to a person they can't afford.

## What it costs

A chief of staff role on your team starts smaller than you'd expect. First AI hire: $29.99/month, 12,000 credits, 7-day free trial.

Credits make the cost legible before you commit. A drafted document is 20–50 credits; a deck is 100–300. You can see what a week of meeting support costs in the same units you buy it in — no token math, no surprise bill.

If the trial produces a week of briefs, summaries, and follow-ups you actually used, the decision makes itself. If it doesn't, you've lost seven days, not a salary.

## Who this is for — and who it isn't

This playbook fits the founder or lean-team lead whose calendar is the bottleneck: solo operators running sales, ops, and support personally, and chiefs of staff at 5–30 person companies who are drowning in coordination.

It doesn't fit everyone. If you enjoy building agent workflows and want to own the architecture, agent-builder tools are the right tool — genuinely; that craft has its place. Kohai is for the other side of that split: people who want the work done, not another system to maintain.

## Start with one meeting

Don't brief Mochi on your whole operation on day one. Pick next Tuesday's hardest meeting — the investor update, the client renewal, the awkward vendor call — and let the flow run once: prep, brief, notes, summary, follow-ups.

You'll know by Wednesday whether Thursday's meetings feel different. That's the whole adoption path: one meeting, one loop, then the rest of the calendar.
