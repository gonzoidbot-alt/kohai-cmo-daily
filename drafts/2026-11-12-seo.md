## Fix 1 — HIGH: Homepage title + description miss every money keyword

Current homepage:
```html
<title>Kōhai | Onboard AI teammates. Get work finished.</title>
<meta name="description" content="Kōhai puts a collaborative AI workspace inside every business, fully staffed with AI teammates from day one. They finish work across any function, and anything irreversible waits for your approval.">
```

Problems: The brand-voice title is nice but contains zero category terms. The queries that convert — "AI employees for small business", "AI workforce platform", "hire AI employees" — appear nowhere in the homepage's strongest on-page signals (title/description/H1). Our own blog post ranks-candidate for "AI for small business" while the homepage — the highest-authority page — competes for nothing. Lindy/Artisan/Sintra all lead with category keywords on their homepages.

Copy-paste replacement (keeps the "onboard AI teammates" voice as the H1's job, puts the category in the title):
```html
<title>AI Employees for Small Business | Kōhai</title>
<meta name="description" content="Kōhai staffs your small business with AI employees — sales, marketing, support, ops — working in the tools you already use. Open-weight and private by default. Start free, no card.">
```
- Title = 39 chars, leads with the money query, brand still present.
- Description = 168 chars (trim "working in the tools you already use" if it renders cut): leads with "AI employees", includes the $0 hook and privacy pillar.
- Leave the H1 ("Onboard AI teammates. Get work finished.") alone — title and H1 splitting brand voice vs. search category is the cheapest fix here.

## Fix 2 — MEDIUM-HIGH: /vs/ comparison pages don't carry the "alternative" query

Current `/vs/lindy`:
```html
<title>Kōhai vs. Lindy: which should you choose?</title>
<meta name="description" content="One AI employee in Slack, priced per person, vs. a whole team on one plan.">
```

Problem: Real search behavior on these pages is "lindy alternative", "lindy pricing", "lindy vs X". The strategy doc (04-content-strategy § SEO/GEO targets) explicitly names "Lindy alternative" and "Artisan alternative" as money queries, and per the competitor analysis Lindy's own blog aggressively publishes head-to-head pricing takedowns to own these queries. Our titles currently match neither the "alternative" nor the "pricing" modifier — and there's no year/price hook for SERP clicks.

Copy-paste for `/vs/lindy` (template for all 16 /vs/ pages):
```html
<title>Lindy Alternative: Kōhai vs. Lindy — Pricing Compared (2026)</title>
<meta name="description" content="Kōhai vs Lindy: a whole AI team on one $20–$200/month plan with unlimited seats, vs. per-user credit pricing. Compare features, privacy and costs side by side.">
```
- Leads with the exact "alternative" query; "(2026)" wins freshness clicks; description contrasts the pricing models factually (Lindy Plus is $29.99/user for 3k credits per our 2026-09-08 capture — verify current Lindy pricing before publishing the comparison body; competitor data in our analysis doc is dated 2026-09-08).
- One-time structural add: redirect `/lindy-alternative` → `/vs/lindy` (currently 404) so the alt-keyword URL exists even if never linked.
