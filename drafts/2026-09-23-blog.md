# From lead list to outreach sequence, without the prospecting grind

Every founder has the same plan for outbound. Build a list of the right companies, write something specific to each one, send it, follow up. And every founder eventually hits the same wall: prospecting is hours of tab-hopping — LinkedIn, the CRM, the company site — for every hour of actual selling.

The work isn't hard. It's just volume: find the company, check the fit, find the person, find the reason to reach out, draft the email, log it all. That's exactly the shape of work an AI colleague should carry. Here's how to hand it over.

## Why outreach is a brief, not a build

Most sales-automation tools start by asking you to construct something: a sequence, a workflow, a decision tree. You spend an afternoon wiring steps before you've written a word to a prospect.

The colleague approach inverts it. On Kohai, you describe the outcome in one message, and the team assembles around the job — a Sales & Growth Lead picks it up with the tools it needs already connected. You never touch a node canvas. Your job is the part only you can do: define who a good lead is.

## The brief is one message

Here's a real one, the kind you'd hand a capable new hire:

> "Build a list of 50 prospects: e-commerce brands under 20 employees, based in the US, with a store that's live but hasn't run a campaign in a while. For each one, find the founder and one specific reason to reach out — a recent product launch, a hiring signal, a new market. Draft a three-touch sequence per prospect, plain and human, no templates. Queue everything for my review before anything sends."

That's the entire job description. No trigger configuration, no field mapping. The research, the list, and the drafts land in your Library as finished work you can read, edit, and approve.

## What your colleague does vs what you do

The division of labor in an outbound pipeline looks like this:

| Pipeline stage | Your AI colleague does | You do |
|---|---|---|
| Research | Finds companies matching your criteria, checks fit signals | Define the criteria |
| List building | Assembles the list in your CRM, deduplicates, enriches | Spot-check the first batch |
| Personalization | Drafts the opening line from real research, one per prospect | Read it — this is where quality shows |
| Sequencing | Drafts all three touches, times the follow-ups | Adjust tone and offer |
| Sending | Holds every send at the approval gate | Approve, edit, or kill |

Two rows deserve attention. Personalization is drafted from actual research — a launch, a job post, a press mention — not a mail-merge field pretending to be one. And sending never happens on its own: outbound touches real people on your behalf, so every send waits at an approval gate until you say go. You stay in control of the messages that leave the building.

## The tool access question is the trust question

Outreach is the workload where access matters most, because a sales colleague touches your CRM, your email account, and third-party data — the same systems that hold every customer relationship you have. This is where per-agent tool scoping carries the weight: **"~1,000 integrations connected day one, with per-agent tool scoping."**

Everything is connected when your team assembles, but connected doesn't mean open. The Sales & Growth Lead sees the CRM and the email drafts; it doesn't see your billing, your codebase, or anything you haven't scoped to it. A colleague that can draft 150 emails but cannot send one without your approval is a colleague you can trust with your pipeline's reputation. Trust comes from the boundary, not from hope.

## What it costs to run this

The math is concrete. In credit terms, outreach drafting is text work — the kind of deliverable priced in the tens of credits per asset, not hundreds. A first month of list-building plus a 50-prospect sequence fits comfortably inside an entry plan, and you can see what a deliverable costs before you brief it.

If the pipeline is a team function rather than a solo one, "Pro tier: $99.99/mo for 40,000 credits and 3 seats" puts a shared pool under the whole GTM crew. Either way, the plan price caps the month before it starts — no usage meter deciding how much prospecting you can afford.

## Start smaller than you think

The failure mode with outbound is the grand launch: a 500-person list, a polished sequence, two weeks of setup, and burnout before the first reply. Skip that. Brief 20 prospects this week. Read every opening line your colleague drafts — the first batch tells you whether the research is good, and re-briefing is one sentence if it isn't.

Approve the sends, log the replies, and you'll know by Friday whether this motion is yours. The list is in your Library, the follow-ups are scheduled, and the only thing you personally touched was the definition of a good lead — which was always the part worth your time.
