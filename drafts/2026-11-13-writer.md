# Lindy Alternative That Doesn't Require Building Workflows — Meet Kōhai

If you've looked at Lindy, you've seen the pitch: AI agents that handle workflows across your tools. It sounds like the answer to every founder's week — until you actually try to set one up.

Because Lindy asks you to *build* first. You design the workflow. You chain the triggers. You map the steps. You're not hiring a teammate — you're architecting a system. And if you're a solo operator or a lean team lead whose week already runs 60 hours, spending another four learning a flow builder is not leverage. It's more work.

There's a different approach. One where you don't build workflows at all. You write one sentence — the same way you'd brief a colleague — and work starts.

It's called Kōhai, and **Start on Kōhai Free — $0, no card, for a limited time — and your first AI teammates are working today.**

## The difference between building and briefing

Lindy positions itself as your "AI workforce," but the first thing you do when you sign up is build a flow. Pick a trigger (new email, calendar event, Slack message), wire it to an action, add conditions, test it, debug it. It's an automation platform wearing an AI hat.

That works great if you enjoy building automations. But for most of the people running a business — the founder doing sales at 9am, support at 11am, content at 2pm, and accounting at 6pm — building automation flows is exactly the kind of work you wanted AI to remove from your plate.

Kōhai flips the model. Instead of *you building the agent*, the agent builds itself.

Here's how a Kōhai user starts their week:

1. Open the chat or the Library.
2. Type: "Prepare the Monday growth report — pipeline, new leads from last week, open support tickets, and a recommendation for the top 3 accounts to reach out to."
3. Kōhai's Strategy Lead pulls from your connected tools — CRM, email, analytics, support desk — and assembles the report.
4. Sensitive actions (sending emails, updating deal stages) pause at an approval gate. You say go, or you say change it.

That's it. No triggers configured. No conditions written. No diagram of boxes and arrows. You briefed it, it did it.

## Why "colleagues, not agents" matters for small teams

Kōhai is built on a simple observation: **you don't want an agent, you want a colleague.** Colleagues come with context. They know which tools they're allowed to touch. They know what needs your approval. They ship finished work, not a half-built draft with a note that says "does this look right?"

A Lindy workflow can do one thing well — email follow-ups, for example. But running your business means sales, support, content, operations, and reporting all happening simultaneously. Each needs its own set of tools, its own permissions, its own working style.

Kōhai assigns named roles — Strategy Lead, Sales & Growth Lead, Marketing & Comms Lead, Customer Support Lead, Operations Lead — each with per-agent tool scoping across ~1,000 integrations. You don't build a workflow for each function. You brief each role the way you'd brief a human teammate, and the colleague adapts.

## The open-weight advantage

Another difference: Lindy runs on multiple models, but the model choice is Lindy's, not yours, and the data story depends on which backend they're routing through.

Kōhai runs exclusively on open-weight models — DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM — from inference providers certified not to train on your data. Per-tenant isolation and encryption are baked in, not bolted on. If your business handles client data (financial records, legal documents, customer PII), this isn't a nice-to-have — it's the difference between being able to use AI at all and being blocked by compliance.

For ICP-3 — the privacy-conscious operator running a finance, legal, or healthcare-adjacent service — this is often the deciding factor. One user put it plainly: "I can't send client data through a model that might train on it. Kōhai's architecture is the only option I've found that doesn't require me to get legal review before every task."

This isn't a takedown of Lindy. Lindy is a solid product for the right user — teams that want to build and manage custom automation flows. Their documentation is excellent. But if you're a solo founder or a lean team lead who needs the *outcome* of automation — reports written, emails sent, leads qualified, tickets answered — without investing time in building the automation itself, Kōhai is a materially different proposition.
