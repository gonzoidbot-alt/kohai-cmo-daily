# What does an AI employee cost? A plain-English pricing guide

Ask five platforms what an AI employee costs and you'll get five different answers, all technically true. One quotes $16 a month. One quotes $600. The difference isn't generosity — it's what each company decided to meter: seats, credits, hours, or actions.

This guide walks through the actual pricing models in the category, what each one means for your bill, and how to estimate your first month before you sign up for anything.

## How much does an AI employee actually cost?

Anywhere from about $16 to $600 per month for self-serve plans, depending on the platform and how much work you hand it. The entry tiers cluster between $24 and $100, and the differences between them matter more than the headline prices.

Here's the honest lay of the land, based on publicly listed pricing as of early September 2026:

## How do the main platforms price AI employees?

| Platform | Self-serve pricing | What's metered |
|---|---|---|
| **Lindy** | Plus $29.99 / Pro $99.99 / Max $199.99 — per user, monthly | Credits per user (3k–35k); pauses when the pool runs low rather than overcharging |
| **Sintra** | $15.60–$48.50/mo on promotional terms (list $97/mo) | 250 monthly credits across all 12 helpers |
| **Marblism** | From $24/mo (yearly) / $39/mo single plan | 50 work-hours/month, all 6 employees included, unlimited team members |
| **Relevance AI** | Free $0 / Pro $19/mo annual / Team $234/mo annual | Actions (tool runs) + pass-through LLM credits you can BYO-key around |
| **Artisan** | Sales-scoped; third-party audits list ~$250–$600/mo annual tiers | Credits, sized to lead volume and mailboxes |
| **Kohai** | $29.99 / $99.99 / $199.99 per plan (not per seat) | Credits per plan: 12k / 40k / 80k |

Credit where it's due, because every one of these companies made a defensible choice. Lindy's pause-don't-overcharge policy genuinely solves the surprise-bill problem. Relevance AI passing LLM costs through at no markup, with rollover and bring-your-own keys, is the most CFO-legible usage pricing in the set. Marblism's "50 hours of work included" is the easiest thing on this list for a non-technical buyer to understand. Sintra's discount ladder gets the entry price below everyone else's. Artisan bundles an entire outbound stack — data, sequencing, dialer, booking — which a horizontal platform doesn't replicate per-vertical.

None of them is a trick. The question is which meter fits how you'll actually use the thing.

## Credits, hours, or actions: which model is fairest?

It depends on what you're buying. Hours (Marblism) are the most intuitive, but two "hours of AI work" can produce wildly different output depending on the task. Actions (Relevance AI) are precise but make you reason about tool runs. Per-user credits (Lindy) work well for one person and multiply awkwardly for a team, because every seat re-buys the pool.

Credits-per-plan, which Kohai uses, meter the work itself rather than the people or the clock. The useful property is that you can price a piece of work before you assign it: a blog post runs 20–50 credits, a deck 100–300. That's the same way you'd think about any hire's capacity — you know what the workload costs before you hand it over.

## What do 40,000 credits actually buy?

A realistic starter month. Drafting and scheduling a week of content, a Monday growth report, an invoice-chase sweep, some support triage, and a research brief or two — that's the kind of load the example-week math on the site is built around, and it fits comfortably inside a mid-tier credit pool.

The ceiling matters too. The credits aren't capped at documents and decks — "App Studio ships deployed software, not mockups," so a landing page or an internal tool comes out of the same pool as the blog posts. One meter, everything from an email draft to a deployed site.

## What should a growing team look for in a plan?

Three things, in order: whether the price is per seat or per plan, how big the work pool is, and what happens when sensitive actions fire.

The per-seat question is where ladders that look identical diverge. Several platforms in this category charge their mid tier per user. Kohai's mid tier is per plan — "Pro tier: $99.99/mo for 40,000 credits and 3 seats." A three-person team shares one pool instead of buying three, and an unlimited-seats tier exists above it for $199.99 if the team keeps growing. If you're comparing platforms at this price point, that seat math is worth doing before anything else.

The work pool is the second check: 250 credits a month gets you familiar with a product; 40,000 gets deliverables shipped. And the third is the approval gate — whichever platform you pick, make sure the sensitive steps (an email to a client, a payment, a deletion) wait for your go before they happen. Kohai builds that in as a default; check that your alternative does too.

## How to estimate your first month in ten minutes

1. Write down the three recurring tasks you'd hand over first — for most founders that's the Monday report, the invoice chase, and the support inbox.
2. Ask each platform you're considering what those tasks consume on their meter: credits, hours, or actions.
3. Compare the pool size against that load, and check whether unused capacity rolls over (Relevance AI's credits roll over while subscribed; several others don't).
4. Check the seat count the price includes before you multiply anything by headcount.
5. Start on the free trial — Lindy, Kohai, Relevance AI, and Artisan all have one — and measure the first deliverable, not the demo.

The platform that prices honestly is the one that can tell you what your month costs before it starts. That's the whole test.
