# What Happens to Your Business When Your AI Vendor Changes Its Mind

You didn't sign up to track an AI vendor's product roadmap. You signed up to get work done — reports assembled, leads contacted, tickets answered. But if the models underneath your AI tools are proprietary and rented, you've quietly taken on a second job: tracking someone else's pricing decisions.

It usually starts small. A usage rate goes up 20%. A feature you relied on moves to a higher tier. An API terms update lands in your inbox with a 30-day window. None of it is malicious — vendors change things. But when every task your AI teammates run routes through one company's proprietary models, every one of those changes hits your operation at once. Your costs, your capabilities, and your data posture are all decided in someone else's boardroom.

There's a structural fix, and it's the reason we built Kōhai on open-weight models.

## Why open-weight changes the math

**Open-weight models only — DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM — from certified no-training providers.** That sentence does three jobs at once, and each one maps to a risk you're probably carrying today.

First, open weights mean no single vendor owns the foundation your workforce runs on. The model architectures are public, weights are published, and multiple certified inference providers can serve them. If one provider changes its pricing or its terms, the work isn't stranded — the same models can run elsewhere. Your operation doesn't renegotiate from a position of zero leverage, because you're not locked to one supplier.

Second, competition among providers serving the same open models keeps the cost of running your team predictable. Proprietary frontier models are priced by one company's judgment of what the market will bear. Open-weight models are priced by a competitive market. For a solo operator, that difference shows up as a bill you can actually forecast.

Third — and this is the part client-data businesses care about most — the inference providers we work with are certified not to train on your data. Combined with per-tenant isolation and encryption, your briefs, your documents, and your customers' information stay yours. "Your data trains our model" isn't a fine-print risk you have to audit; it's not in the deal at all.

## The three risks you carry on proprietary models

If you're running your AI work on rented, closed models, here's the exposure, laid out plainly:

| Risk | What it looks like | How open-weight answers it |
|---|---|---|
| Pricing drift | Usage rates climb; your AI bill becomes the line item you dread opening | Competitive provider market, not one vendor's price sheet |
| Capability whiplash | A model you built workflows around gets deprecated or gated | Published weights; the same models served by multiple providers |
| Data posture drift | Terms of service update; training-on-your-data language appears in a revision | Certified no-training providers, per-tenant isolation, encryption |

Notice that two of the three aren't privacy problems at all — they're pricing and continuity problems. That's deliberate. Open-weight isn't just an ethics position; it's an economics position. The same structural choice that protects your data also protects your margins.

## What predictability looks like in practice

Predictability isn't a feeling — it's a number you can write down before you commit. On Kōhai, work is priced in credits, and the ranges are published: small tasks like a research pull or an email draft run 5–10 credits, medium tasks like a blog post or a landing page run 20–50, and large deliverables like a deck or a research report run 100–300. If a task fails, it consumes 0 credits. You can see what a week of briefed work costs before you assign it — and because the models underneath are open-weight and competitively served, the underlying economics don't move just because one company had a hard quarter.

The plans follow the same logic. Kōhai Free is $0, no card required — limited time, and the first 1,000 users keep it. Kōhai is $20/month for 8,000 credits. Kōhai Pro is $100/month for 40,000 credits. Kōhai Max is $200/month for 80,000 credits. Unlimited seats on every plan — your whole team works from the same subscription, and every role template and integration is included rather than metered per seat.

That's four numbers on a page, not a calculator and a prayer.

## The independence dividend

Here's the part that compounds. When you brief an AI colleague on Kōhai, you're not just buying this week's output — you're building a working relationship: the briefs you write, the workflows your colleagues settle into, the deliverables in your Library. On proprietary infrastructure, all of that institutional knowledge sits on top of someone else's roadmap. A model retirement or a terms change doesn't just raise a rate; it threatens the working system you spent months building.

On open-weight models, the system you build keeps its foundation even as individual providers come and go. Your briefs still work. Your colleagues still run. The thing you built stays built.

For a solo operator, that's the difference between an AI tool and an AI team. Tools get repriced out from under you. Teams keep working.

## The honest trade-off

We'll say it straight: open-weight models trade some frontier-model prestige for independence, predictability, and privacy. If you enjoy chasing the newest proprietary model the week it ships, this isn't built for you — and there are fine products that are. But if you're running a business where the work needs to keep shipping — where an AI bill surprise is a budget problem and a data-training clause is a client problem — the trade isn't close. Predictable economics, a competitive model market, and a no-training guarantee beat a logo on a benchmark chart.

Your first AI hire shouldn't come with a second job tracking the vendor. Start on Kōhai Free — $0, no card, limited time — and brief your first teammate this afternoon. The first deliverable lands in your Library today.
